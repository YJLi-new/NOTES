After reading Kurt Vonnegut’s *How to Write with Style*, I found that the advice “Find a subject you care about” is the easiest for me to apply. However, I don’t choose a subject just to grab the reader’s attention. When I’m truly interested in a topic, I can use the style, mood, characters, and setting related to it more freely, which makes it easier to write. This also helps me focus more on the words and structure of my work, giving me a sense of freedom in creating.

Besides Vonnegut’s seven tips, I believe it is also very important for a writer to truly understand their own writing style—this means knowing how you write and what kind of work you want to create. If you want to write what you consider your “best piece,” you have to know what is a “good piece” in your own view. Only by figuring out how you arrange words and where you place emphasis can you improve your style, explore your potential, and avoid some possible obstacles.

I won’t deny that works created from emotion or sudden inspiration can sometimes turn out great. But on the one hand, for most writers, that approach tends to be more random and requires a lot of writing experience to master. On the other hand, after discovering and using the writer's own principles, the process will be more efficient and focused.

Vladimir Nabokov’s *Pale Fire* is a work I particularly learn a lot from. Its carefully designed structure keeps me thinking about the nature of a text and the meaning of how readers interpret it, deepening my understanding of Nabokov’s emphasis on paying attention to the text itself. In addition, the poetic quality, word choice, and rhythm in this work reveal Nabokov’s focus on the musicality of language and his unique aesthetic sense about it. This has inspired me to pay closer attention to these elements in my own writing.
