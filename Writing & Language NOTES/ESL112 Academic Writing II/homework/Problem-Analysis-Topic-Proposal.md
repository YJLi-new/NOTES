# Problem Analysis Essay Topic Proposal

## Overview

For this assignment, you will propose two possible topics for the Problem Analysis Essay. These topics should be interesting to you and should meet the requirements of the assignment set out in the prompt. Your instructor will review this proposal and provide feedback. In this feedback, your instructor will approve one or both of your topics (possibly with some revision) or suggest finding a new topic.

### Step 1 – Review the Assignment Prompt

Choose a problem in a society (e.g., a community, a state, a region, a country) affecting a specific population (e.g., an ethnic or cultural group, an age or gender group, an animal species) and **analyze the causes <u>and/or</u> effects of the problem**. Your analysis should be based on evidence from reliable sources.

Breaking Down the Prompt:

1.  Find a significant world problem (e.g., “malnutrition in South Sudan”)

2.  Browse the web and library resources to learn about it in general (use “journalist questions” like Who?, What?, Where?, When?, Why?, & How?). 

3.  Then, look at it more specifically (What are its causes? Effects? Potential solutions?)

### Step 2 – Questions to Guide Your Brainstorming

Consider the following questions as you come up with ideas for your paper. 

1.  Is this problem/issue **<u>significant</u>**? Does it demand action be taken to deal with it? 

2.  Is this potential topic of **<u>appropriate scope</u>**? It should not be too narrow (i.e. not enough to discuss in the paper) nor too broad (i.e. too much to cover, too vague, too many sources). \***Reminder**: you have 3 to 4 pages (not counting references) to write this paper.

3.  Is this problem/issue **<u>current</u>**? Is it something that people are still working on today?  

4.  Is this problem **<u>solvable</u>**? Are there possible solutions to this problem?  

#### Additional Pre-Research/Brainstorming Suggestions

1.  **Propose *Unique* Ideas**: pick something you find interesting! The goal is for everyone in class to write about their own topic. 

2.  **Places to Start Researching**: since this is essentially the brainstorming phase of the research process, try to find information in a wide variety of places, including the [**<u>library databases</u>**](https://www.library.illinois.edu/), [**<u>Wikipedia</u>**](https://www.wikipedia.org/), [**<u>CQ Researcher</u>**](https://library.cqpress.com/cqresearcher/), reputable news organizations like the New York Times and The Guardian, and/or your favorite search engines. 

3.  **Ask Questions!** If you’re not sure about something, send your instructor an email, a message on Canvas, or stop by their office hours sooner rather than later! 

### Step 3 – Topic Proposals

Answer the following questions for each potential topic. You should propose two possible topics. For questions 1 and 2, each answer should be a short paragraph in length. Please write your answers **in a different color** below the question prompts. 

**\*Note:** you are proposing \*potential\* topic ideas. This activity is designed to get you thinking critically about possible topic ideas that meet the assignment requirements and then justify why these potential topics might work for this assignment. Your instructor should approve a topic before you can begin further research/writing.
