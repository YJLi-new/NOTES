## Part I: Introduction

1.  Problem

> Germany's food banks are collapsing due to rising demand and falling supply

2.  Community that it affects

> The problem primarily affects vulnerable groups, including low-income families, refugees, working-class people, and a growing number of middle-class families.

3.  Provide some brief information about why the problem is important. How widespread is the issue? What impact does it have? (Borrow from your Problem Analysis essay)

    Germany's food banks, once a temporary support system for the most vulnerable, are now gradually collapsing due to increased demand and reduced supply. Originally set up as temporary relief organizations during the recession, these food banks have evolved into long-term safety nets as the cost of living exceeds wages and social support systems struggle to keep up. Supporters of the status quo argue that government welfare provisions coupled with food bank services are sufficient to prevent widespread hunger. Critics, however, argue that the increasing reliance on charity points to a systemic inadequacy that fails to address the root causes of poverty. The issue is not only important for those already living on the economic margins; when middle-class families begin queuing up at food banks, it is a sign that social rifts are deepening and could foreshadow even greater economic and social challenges ahead for Germany. The German food bank crisis was caused by inflation, rising immigration, and falling donations, leading to rising food insecurity and greater social inequality.
