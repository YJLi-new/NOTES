My Favorite Gaming Studio

> When people talk about what video games are, some may say that they’re a way to gain interest, like Tencent’s games; others may say that they’re toys, like Nintendo’s games. However, ZA/UM, an indie game developing studio from Estonia, says that: video games are a new kind of art and a new way out for literature. The studio’s unique idea of game playing and its spirit of humanity and literature attracts me so profoundly that ZA/UM has become my favorite gaming studio.
>
> ZA/UM has only published one game, *Disco Elysium*, so far, but I was shocked when I first glimpsed the artistic introduction of the game, “the last letter from the Soviet Union”. Nowadays, people’s free time is becoming more and more fragmented. As a result, the video games tend to provide short but intense stimuli instead of the immersive experience in old-fashioned games. Also, long novels are losing their readers for the same reason. However, in such a bad environment, ZA/UM can say: “Nobody is reading now, then why not try the video games?” Then they made *Disco Elysium* become a game that almost presents the experience beyond traditional literature. They also brought ZA/UM itself was brought into my heart.
>
> Regarding video games, members of ZA/UM seem to believe that players can get stronger feelings and understand the characters better by making their own choices and interacting in video games. They showed the opinion obviously in *Disco Elysium*. In the fictional city, Revacholian, there are all kinds of people with various beliefs, philosophical perspectives, attitudes toward life, and even accents. The rich of their backgrounds enable players to experience a society with complicated culture and “the ghost of communism”. The social environment is similar to what Eastern Europeans are experiencing in reality now. Also, they removed the combat plot and limited the gaming environment to a small site. That design complicates and deepens the connections among all characters. Without the purpose of gameplay guiding, the freedom of the players’ own stories was powerfully increased. That also deepens players’ emotional empathy with the main character. This style that focuses on the narrative and decreases gameplay has always been something I expect in video games.
>
> By the way, the art of *Disco Elysium* has an eye-catching style of the Russian Ambulant School combined with Modern Western Art. The excellent art design makes the literary elements more intuitive and enriches my emotional experience in the game. Hence, I cherish this unique *Disco Elysium* and the ZA/UM that created it so much.
>
> Beyond the game, the ZA/UM’s artistic temperament also reaches my heart. Among ZA/UM, some of the members are artists, writers, musicians, and even university professors. The founder of ZA/UM called ZA/UM an art association rather than a company. In the studio’s name, “ZA” stands for “exceed” and “UM” for “rationality”. That meaning shows their pursuit of philosophy. Before the creation of *Disco Elysium*, the core creators had been a group of friends. Together, they had formed a rock band and played Tabletop Role-Playing Games. They also had published a failed novel. Because of ZA/UM’s passion for art and philosophy activities, *Disco Elysium* emphasizes an impact on inner philosophy ideas. It also generates an understanding of capitalism. Meanwhile, players can find dark humor throughout the game. Those contents create serious literature textures. *Disco Elysium* also presents multiple points of view on the social history of Eastern Europe by using the fictional history of Revacholian. Such thoughtful content is what I expect to see in a game worth playing and in-depth literature. The studio’s art-related history and the game’s content engage me to look back and think about *Disco Elysium* constantly. The more I think about it, the more I respect ZA/UM.
>
> Unfortunately, an official announcement was released by ZA/UM a few days ago. The core creators of ZA/UM were all fired by the company’s shareholders. That may indicate the death of my favorite ZA/UM. Though *Disco Elysium*’s story hasn’t been finished, the ending of ZA/UM finally gives the game, which criticizes capitalism, a dramatic ending.
>
> Even though the brave ZA/UM has gone, I must thank them for bringing me such a wonderful gaming time. It has improved my understanding of modern art and my knowledge of modern politics and philosophy. More importantly, their attempts to combine literature and games have given me more confidence in the future development of games into art. *Disco Elysium* does make my life better.
>
> The valuable thought ZA/UM tried to convey and the creative attempt they made won’t fade away, nor my love for ZA/UM.
