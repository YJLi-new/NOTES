The New York Times

by Jan Hoffman

Hello everyone. Today I’m going to give my presentation about the article *How Anti-Vaccine Sentiment Took Hold in the United States* by Jan Hoffman.

In the article, the author talked about the three reasons anti-vaccine sentiment took hold in the USA.

The first reason is that,

By using vaccines, many diseases have been prevented successfully. But the vanish of theses disease leads people to forget the danger of the diseases, then the benefit of vaccines are also forgotten because people have been used to the society without the prevented diseases.

The second reason is that,

Some scientific articles claim that the vaccine had horrifying side effects. Although they were proven wrong and retracted, these views have had an impact on people’s attitude to vaccines and increased anti-vaccine sentiment through social media.

The last reason is that,

Pediatricians and agencies such as the C.D.C. are losing their credibility in parents. It also lead to many anti-vaccine groups, which contribute a lot to anti-vaccine sentiment.

反疫苗情绪高涨降低了疫苗接种率，导致部分危险疾病的疫苗接种率低于群体免疫的最低要求，可能引起这些疾病重新爆发，危害公众健康。

The rise in anti-vaccine sentiment has reduced vaccination rates, which may cause the dangerous diseases return and endanger public health.

And since we have known why anti-vaccine sentiment is raising in United States, how can we do to reduce it?

Well, some may say that, if people don’t want vaccination, just let the government, the polices, force them to do it!

But I think, 强迫行为大概率doesn’t work and may cause many negative 后果.

But I think, coercive policies possibly doesn't work and may cause many negative consequences.

We should reduce anti-vaccine sentiment in a gentle, accessible way

As liberalism has become popular among citizens in modern society, forced vaccination violates people's freedom to control their own bodies and may cause discontent and opposition among the public, and increase instability of society. The disadvantage of forced vaccinations is larger than its advantage.

如果真的颁布强迫接种疫苗的法律法规，基层管理者，比如警察，的执法合法性将会成为另一个问题。

If we truly release laws about compulsory vaccination, the legality of law enforcement will be another issue.

According to statistics from a relevant institution, it only takes about 700 hours to train an American policeman.

一旦执法人员受到的监督较弱，不完全按照法律执行，比如强迫不适合接种疫苗的人接种疫苗，将会切实地损害民众的健康安全，比病毒更甚。

Once law enforcement officers are under weak supervision and do not fully follow the law, such as forcing people who are not suitable for vaccination to be vaccinated, it will actually damage the health and safety of the people, even worse than the virus.

既然强迫接种无法实行，我们或许可以选取一些更温和、更人性化的方式。

Since forced vaccination is not practical, we may take some gentler and milder methods.

To improve 接种疫苗率 effectively and solve the anti-vaccine sentiment 彻底地, we should make 疫苗相关的宣传机构更可信, and provide more details of vaccines’ effect beyond data.

To improve vaccination rates effectively and solve the anti-vaccine sentiment thoroughly, we should make vaccine-related agencies more credible, and provide more details of vaccines' effect beyond just numbers.

Hoffman thinks that, "people tend to believe an individual's \_narrative over abstract numbers." Indeed, besides the numbers and the files, we need some detailed stories to prove the effectiveness of vaccines.

For example, 为了证明接种脊髓灰质炎疫苗的必要性，我们先来证明接种脊髓灰质炎疫苗的有效性。【小儿麻痹症症状】【小儿麻痹症后遗症、死亡人数（得病后人群生存状况）】【社会影响】【脊髓灰质炎疫苗接种】【大规模接种后，当代儿童生活变更自由】【小儿麻痹症传播现状】

For example, in order to prove the need for polio vaccination, we first need to prove the effectiveness of polio vaccination. \[Polio symptoms\] \[Polio sequelae and deaths (survival of the population after the disease)\] \[Social impact\] \[Polio vaccination\] \[Changes in the lives of contemporary children after mass vaccination\] \[Current status of polio transmission\].

通过这些具体、现实的真实事件，人们能更好地理解并接受疫苗接种。

Through these specific, realistic stories, people can better understand and accept vaccination.

同时，为了让人们信服这些信息的可靠性，发布信息的权威机构必须在日常注意维护自身名誉。

At the same time, in order to convince people of the reliability of the information, the related agencies must pay attention to improving their own reputation.

我认为，人们之所以比起专家发表的科普文章更相信反疫苗团体说的话，是因为在人们的认知中，他们和这个专家根本不熟悉，没见过他一面、更没跟他说过话。相反地，人们往往能在现实中见到反疫苗团体并与他们亲自交谈。人们更倾向于信任他们认为自己熟悉的人的论述，而论述之中的逻辑与正确性其实是次要的。可惜的是，当今的疫苗科普工作多注重于后者，造成了减少反疫苗情绪的效果不佳。

I think the reason people believe what anti-vaccine groups say more than popular science articles published by experts is that in people's views, they are not familiar with this expert at all, have never met him, let alone talked to him . Instead, people can often meet anti-vaccine groups in real life and talk to them in person. People are more inclined to trust the statements of people they think they know well, and the logic and correctness of the statements are actually secondary. It is a pity that today's vaccine science popularization work focuses more on the latter, resulting in poor results in reducing anti-vaccine sentiment.

因此，如果权威机构，加强对自身的宣传，提升在民间的声誉，对疫苗接种的宣传科普自然会有效果。

Therefore, if agencies, pay attention to improving their public reputation, the popularization of vaccination will naturally become effective.

疫苗为人类健康做出的的贡献是不可忽视的。我相信只要采取正确的宣传措施，不科学的反疫苗情绪一定会被削减。

The contribution of vaccines to human health cannot be ignored. I believe that as long as the correct propaganda measures are taken, the unscientific anti-vaccine sentiment will definitely decline.
