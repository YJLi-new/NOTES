To discuss my English writing experience, I might first say something about my longest English writing practice experience so far. This was in junior high school and high school, I used to practice writing one short English essay almost every day. Those essays were all explanatory, assumed to be applied in some situations, like writing a letter explaining why someone must be absent from school for a day. However, usually, I only needed to follow some fixed templates to get high scores. When I came to the university, I just wrote a few more English essays for the General Education courses. Sometimes, I tried translating some script works from my community online into English. At other times, I would write a lot of short English emails to communicate with the university and the professors. But obviously, these writings were neither academic nor formal. Thus, the experiences did not significantly enhance my English writing skills.

Anyhow, reflecting on these experiences, I now have some understanding of English writing for myself. It is kind of simple for me in some parts, and relatively hard for me in some other parts. For me, grammar may be the easiest part of English writing. The English grammar rules are usually simple and clear, and most of the word forms follow a certain pattern. Consequently, it is relevant simple to understand and use them, even for me a beginner in English writing. In contrast, as a non-native English speaker who has never lived in any English-speaking community, those things involving English vocabulary are quite unfamiliar to me and difficult to memorize, like the word combinations, the complex meanings behind each word, and the meanings of the words when used together. Such a language gap makes it hard for me to convey some emotions and meanings precisely in English.

In addition to the language gap, the cultural gap also causes difficulties for me to write, in English, and in the U.S. In my perspective, what I write is based on what I think, and that is be deeply affected by the culture I experience in my daily life. Moving to the U.S., I will need to make efforts to keep the way I write like I did in China. This caused some difficulties for me and took some time to get used to.

The difficulties are truly bothering me, and I eagerly want to get used to them as soon as possible, as writing is really important to me. In school, I need to write emails for communication, and I will need to write academic essays. More importantly, in my leisure time, writing is one of my main hobbies. I read literature; I study literature criticism; I write my works. Writing has brought me a lot of joy, but it is almost all in Chinese.

Although I have come into this English-speaking community at UIUC, I still prefer to use my native language for writing, which is Chinese. This preference of mine is mainly based on my better proficiency in Chinese than English, which means the conversion from my thoughts to the text is more efficient using Chinese. For instance, the rhyme pattern after each character in Chinese is easy and clear to follow for me, so I can freely modify the pronunciation of a sentence by choosing some other characters I want. However, in English, I have to change the words or form of the words to modify them, which are what I am unfamiliar with. Besides, there is another reason that Chinese is a more analytic language compared to English. In practice, the grammar rules often play a small role. The choice of characters in a Chinese essay can be very free. By arranging the components in various ways, some very interesting effects can be created in a sentence.

As a result of the above, I’m now hoping to solve my difficulties in English writing by learning it, also eager to discover more features and advantages, bridging the gap between my Chinese and English writing abilities. In this course ESL111, I will spend enough time and effort to chase my goal, and be excited to see where this course will lead me.
