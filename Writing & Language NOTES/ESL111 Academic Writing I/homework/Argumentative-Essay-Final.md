**Students should be allowed to take mental health days off**

Since the beginning of the pandemic, rates of depression and anxiety among adolescents have doubled (Bruce, 2022). Such an alarming increase has made people more worried about students' mental health, prompting several states to consider the implementation of policies that would allow students to take mental health days off from school. Mental health is now being recognized as an important part of overall health and success in school. People who support mental health days say that they provide much-needed respite and support for students' well-being. Conversely, the opponents argue that such days off encourage avoidance and fail to foster resilience. In my opinion, high school students should be allowed to take mental health days off from school. This policy would help students take care of their mental health by giving them a break from the pressures of school and a chance to gain emotional value and support outside the school.

Allowing students to take mental health days off is a good way to help them deal with their mental health and take a break from school stress. One of the most important needs for young people is being able to take breaks from studying or working. A 2020 Mental Health America survey revealed that the most frequently cited needs among 14- to 18-year-olds were access to mental health professionals and the inclusion of mental health absences or breaks as part of school or work (Bruce, 2022). This suggests that students know how important it is to have time to take care of their mental health. However, there is a shortage of mental health professionals and the services they offer. Nearly half the nation lives in a designated mental health worker shortage area" (Bruce, 2022). Even in areas where professionals are available, "they often do not accept public insurance, making them inaccessible to many kids" (Bruce, 2022). In this situation, mental health days can help students manage stress, avoid burnout, and keep up with their schoolwork when they can't get professional help. Some people refute that letting students take mental health days teaches them to avoid problems, which could make anxiety and depression worse over time. Like Daniel Buck, he states that "if you want to help someone with anxiety or fear, avoidance will only worsen the problem" (Buck, 2021). He thinks that the mental health days make students avoid challenges instead of building resilience. However, mental health professionals show the significance of rest as an important element in the process of mental health recovery. The American Psychological Association espouses the value of self-care, including the practice of taking breaks when necessary. They point out that self-care can help to reduce stress and anxiety while improving happiness. It's a mistake to think that rest is the same as avoidance. It actually gives students the chance to deal with their mental health problems in a proactive way. When students get a break from the pressure of their studies, they're better able to tackle the challenges of anxiety and depression. This allows them to take meaningful action to address these issues.

Moreover, allowing students to take mental health days off make them able to meet their social and emotional needs outside of the school environment. This is particularly beneficial for those who are experiencing negative school climates. Schools can, at times, serve as a source of stress and negative emotional states due to the presence of bullying, peer pressure, or toxic relationships. Mental health days let students step away from harmful situations so they can recover and get help. For instance, "Her classmate Ariyonnah Brown, 14, said she took a day to help defuse a situation with another student... 'It's gotten overwhelming at times'" (Bruce, 2022). This example demonstrates how time away from school can assist students in dealing with conflicts that add to their stress. When students are away from school, they can also connect with family or community resources that can help them, which is good for their mental health recovery.

In conclusion, high school students should be permitted to take mental health days off in order to address their mental health needs and support their overall well-being. Mental health days provide students with a break from academic stress, helping them manage stress and prevent burnout. They also give students a chance to meet their social and emotional needs outside of school, especially if the school environment is harmful. By teaching students’ healthy ways to cope, we can address concerns about promoting avoidance. By adopting these policies, we can help create a generation of students who are healthier, more resilient, and better prepared to face future challenges. It is crucial to give students time to focus on their mental health, which helps them in the short term and also helps them develop stress management and self-care skills that will last a lifetime. By making mental health a priority, we show students that their health matters and give them the tools they need to succeed in school and in life while maintaining good mental health.
