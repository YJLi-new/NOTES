In recent years, mental health issues among students have been increasing, especially since the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic. In response, some states are implementing policies that allow students to take time off, The article "More states are allowing students to take mental health days off" by Giles Bruce discusses it, aiming to show that while more states are implementing policies that allow students to take time off, these measures alone are insufficient to address the potential youth mental health crisis due to a lack of adequate mental health services and resources in schools. Bruce's purpose is to highlight the difference between policy implementation and mental health support availability and the importance of taking comprehensive solutions. Through the effective use of logos, ethos, and pathos, Bruce successfully supports his main argument, convincing his target audience of educators, administrators, parents, and mental health professionals.

In the article, Bruce (2022) successfully implements the logos by providing convincing statistics and evidence. He used it to point out the severity of the youth mental health crisis and the inadequacy of current resources. Stating that “seventy percent of schools that responded to a federal survey in April said more students had sought mental health services since the pandemic started” (Bruce, 2022, para. 5), he powerfully illustrates the significant increase in demand for mental health services among students since the pandemic's onset. By citing data from a federal survey, Bruce appeals to the audience's sense of reason and provides credible evidence that shows the urgency of addressing mental health issues in schools. Furthermore, Bruce (2022) notes that "Nearly half the nation lives in a designated mental health worker shortage area... and an estimated 7,550 new professionals are needed to fill that void nationwide" (para. 5). This fact emphasizes the widespread shortage of mental health professionals, highlighting a critical problem about the need for services and the resources available. By presenting this alarming data, Bruce not only strengthens his logical appeal but also warns the audience to recognize the systemic challenges hindering effective mental health support for students. Overall, Bruce's strategic use of logos effectively persuades his target audience by providing solid evidence. This greatly strengthens his argument that schools are in urgent need of comprehensive mental health resources.

Moreover, ethos is used by Bruce (2022) when he utilized the experts’ authority to build trust with his audience. For example, Bruce (2022) quotes Tamar Mendelson, director of the Center for Adolescent Health at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, who states, "The more we can shift to a prevention mindset and integrate mental health promotion into schools from a young age, I see that as very key to helping reduce the needs for treatment that we see in young people" (para. 10). This quote improved Bruce's ethical appeal because Mendelson is a respected expert in adolescent health. By citing her professional opinion, Bruce adds weight to his argument, demonstrating that authoritative and credible sources support it. This strategy builds trust with the audience, as they are more likely to be persuaded by thoughts from recognized experts in the relevant field. At the same time, Bruce (2022) includes the perspective of veteran teacher Ben Lobo who said: "I've been a teacher for 19 years, and this is as bad as I've seen it" (para. 12). Lobo’s statement contributes to this spirit by providing the personal experience of an educator who is very familiar with student mental health issues. His extensive teaching experience lends credibility to his observations of deteriorating student mental health. By highlighting Lobo’s insights, Bruce further enhances his credibility by aligning his argument with the experience of an experienced professional. Therefore, Bruce effectively uses ethos to strengthen his argument, establishing trust with his audience through authoritative sources and experienced professionals, which convince his target audience to recognize the necessity of comprehensive mental health support in schools.

Furthermore, Bruce powerfully employs pathos through personal stories and true storytelling, making his readers empathize with him then trust him. For instance, Bruce (2022) quotes a 17-year-old student: "I'm somebody who struggles with my mental health quite a bit... When you're in school and not fully mentally there, it's like you're not really grasping anything anyway" (para. 2). Such emotionally-laden narratives call for empathy in the reader. It allows them to relate to the student’s daily struggles with mental problems. By using real-life experience, Bruce makes the mental health crisis more practical, strengthening the emotional appeal of his argument. Besides, Bruce (2022) includes the testimony from Sheila Blanco. She shared that "parents need to be educated... So many parents, they don't know what resources are, and even if there are resources, to help out the child or help them to help the child" (Bruce, 2022, para. 24). Blanco tragically lost her daughter to suicide, which not only leaves a strong emotional impression on the audience but also powerfully shows the serious consequences of inadequate mental health support, making the argument more compelling. Hence, Bruce's adept use of ethos effectively engages the audience's sympathy and concern, reinforcing the necessity for comprehensive mental health support in schools. It fits well into his overall persuasive strategy.

Bruce uses all three rhetorical appeals - logos, ethos, and pathos - to construct a persuasive argument. Logical appeals provide strong evidence for the mental health crisis and the inadequacy of current resources, making the argument intellectually compelling. Ethical appeals build credibility by incorporating expert testimony and professional insights, thereby establishing trust with the audience. Emotional appeals humanize the issue through personal stories, making it emotionally resonant. Bruce successfully highlights the gap between policy implementation and the availability of mental health support in schools. He achieves this by effectively engaging his target audience with logical evidence, reliable sources, and emotional connections, emphasizing the need for comprehensive solutions to the youth mental health crisis. However, while Bruce adeptly employs rhetorical appeals, the article could benefit from a more balanced discussion by addressing potential counterarguments or exploring alternative solutions to the mental health crisis.
