**1. Whole number times a fraction**

Example: 6 x 2/3

> Story problem: Mia filled 6 planters with 2/3 of a pound of soil in each planter. How many total pounds of soil did she use?
>
> Estimate: Each planter has less than 1 pound of soil, so the total should be less than 6 pounds.
>
> Work:
>
> 6 x 2/3 means 6 groups of 2/3.
>
> 2/3 + 2/3 + 2/3 + 2/3 + 2/3 + 2/3 = 12/3
>
> 12/3 = 4
>
> Answer: 4.

This makes sense because every 3 thirds makes 1 whole pound. Six groups of 2/3 gives 12 thirds, which equals 4 whole pounds.

**2. Fraction times a whole number**

Example: 2/3 x 6

> Story problem: Mia had 6 planters. She planted tomatoes in 2/3 of them. How many planters had tomatoes?
>
> Work:
>
> 2/3 of 6 means divide the 6 planters into 3 equal groups and take 2 of the groups.
>
> 6 divided into 3 equal groups gives 2 planters in each group.
>
> 2 groups of 2 planters = 4 planters
>
> Answer: **4**

This example shows that 6 x 2/3 and 2/3 x 6 have the same answer, but the meanings are slightly different. The first means 6 groups of 2/3. The second means 2/3 of 6.

**3. Fraction times fraction**

Example: 2/3 x 3/4

> Story problem: Devin had 3/4 of a pizza left in the fridge. He ate 2/3 of the leftovers for breakfast. How much of the original whole pizza did he eat?
>
> Work:
>
> 2/3 x 3/4 means 2/3 of 3/4.
>
> If 3/4 of the pizza was left, that means 3 fourth-size pieces were left. Devin ate 2/3 of those 3 pieces, so he ate 2 of the fourth-size pieces.
>
> 2 fourths = 2/4
>
> 2/4 = 1/2
>
> Answer: 1/2
>
> Using the algorithm: 2/3 x 3/4 = 6/12; 6/12 = 1/2

The visual model and the algorithm give the same answer.

**4. Multiplication on a number line**

Example: 1/2 x 5/6

> Story problem: On Monday, I ran 5/6 of a mile. On Tuesday, I ran 1/2 of that distance. How many miles did I run on Tuesday?
>
> Estimate: 5/6 is less than 1 mile. Half of 5/6 must be less than 1/2 mile.
>
> Work: 1/2 x 5/6 = 5/12
>
> Answer: **5/12**

A number line can show this by first marking 5/6, then finding half of that distance. To split 5/6 in half, the sixths can be divided into twelfths.

**5. Important meaning of fraction multiplication**

In fraction multiplication, the first fraction usually means “of” the second fraction.

Example: 1/2 x 5/6 means **1/2 of 5/6**.

The 5/6 and the answer 5/12 are both measured in miles. But the 1/2 does not mean 1/2 of a mile. It means half of the 5/6-mile distance.

This is different from fraction addition and subtraction. In addition and subtraction, the fractions usually need to refer to the same whole. In multiplication, the first fraction often acts on the second fraction.

**6. Brownie pan model**

Example: 1/2 x 1/3

> Story problem: Adam came home and found 1/3 of a brownie pan left. He ate 1/2 of those leftovers. How much of the whole brownie pan did he eat?
>
> Work: 1/2 x 1/3 means 1/2 of 1/3.
>
> Answer: **1/6**
>
> Algorithm: 1/2 x 1/3 = 1/6
>
> The wording matters. The question should ask how much of the **whole brownie pan** he ate, not just how many brownies he ate, because “how many brownies” depends on how the pan was cut.

**7. Mixed number multiplication**

Example: 1/2 x 2 3/4

> Story problem: Kirsten had 2 3/4 pounds of tofu. She gave 1/2 of it to Becky. How many pounds of tofu did Becky get?
>
> Estimate: Half of almost 3 pounds should be a little less than 1 1/2 pounds.
>
> Work: 2 3/4 = 11/4; 1/2 x 11/4 = 11/8; 11/8 = 1 3/8
>
> Answer: **1 3/8.**

This answer makes sense because 1 3/8 is a little less than 1 1/2.

**8. Mixed number times mixed number**

Example: 1 1/4 x 1 2/3

> Story problem: One batch of chili needs 1 2/3 pounds of pinto beans. I want to make 1 1/4 batches of chili. How many pounds of pinto beans do I need?
>
> Work: 1 1/4 = 5/4; 1 2/3 = 5/3; 5/4 x 5/3 = 25/12; 25/12 = 2 1/12
>
> Answer: **2 1/12.**

The visual model can show one full batch plus 1/4 of another batch. The algorithm confirms the answer.

**9. Whole number divided by a fraction**

Example: 4 divided by 1/3

> Story problem: I have 4 dog biscuits. I break each biscuit into thirds. How many 1/3-size pieces do I have?
>
> Work:
>
> 4 divided by 1/3 means: How many groups of 1/3 are in 4?
>
> Each whole biscuit has 3 thirds.
>
> 4 biscuits x 3 pieces each = 12 pieces
>
> Answer: 12.

**10. Division as measurement division**

Example: 3 divided by 3/8

> Story problem: I have 3 yards of ribbon. It takes 3/8 of a yard to make one bow tie. How many bow ties can I make?
>
> Work: 3 divided by 3/8 means: How many groups of 3/8 are in 3?
>
> Change 3 yards into eighths: 3 = 24/8
>
> Now see how many groups of 3/8 fit into 24/8: 24/8 divided by 3/8 = 8
>
> Answer: 8

This is a measurement division problem because I know the total amount and the size of each group. I am trying to find the number of groups.

**11. Important meaning of fraction division**

In fraction division, the first two numbers usually refer to the same whole, but the answer tells the number of groups.

Example: 3 divided by 3/8

> The 3 means 3 yards of ribbon.\
> The 3/8 means 3/8 of a yard per bow tie.\
> The answer, 8, means 8 bow ties.
>
> So the answer is not yards. The answer is the number of groups that can be made.

**12. Fraction divided by fraction**

Example: 3/4 divided by 1/2

> Story problem: A serving size is 1/2 of a cookie. How many servings can I make from 3/4 of a cookie?
>
> Work:
>
> 3/4 divided by 1/2 means: How many 1/2-size servings are in 3/4?
>
> 3/4 contains one full 1/2 serving, with 1/4 of a cookie left.
>
> Since 1/4 is half of 1/2, the leftover amount is 1/2 of another serving.

3/4 divided by 1/2 = 1 1/2.

> Answer: 1 1/2.

**13. Another division model**

Example: 5/8 divided by 1/2

> Story problem: A serving size is 1/2 of a cookie. How many servings can I make from 5/8 of a cookie?
>
> Estimate: 5/8 is more than 1/2, so the answer should be more than 1 serving but less than 2 servings.
>
> Work:
>
> 1/2 = 4/8
>
> 5/8 contains one full 4/8 serving, with 1/8 left.
>
> The leftover 1/8 is 1/4 of another 1/2 serving because 1/2 = 4/8.
>
> 5/8 divided by 1/2 = 1 1/4.
>
> Answer: **1 1/4.**

**14. Dividing fractions with fraction tiles**

Example: 1/2 divided by 1/8

> Question: How many 1/8 pieces fit into 1/2?
>
> Work:
>
> 1/2 = 4/8
>
> So four 1/8 pieces fit into 1/2.
>
> Answer:
>
> 1/2 divided by 1/8 = 4

**18. Another fraction division example**

> Example: 3/8 divided by 1/4
>
> Question: How many 1/4-size groups fit into 3/8?
>
> Work:
>
> 1/4 = 2/8
>
> 3/8 contains one full 2/8 group, with 1/8 left.
>
> The leftover 1/8 is half of 1/4.
>
> Answer: 3/8 divided by 1/4 = 1 1/2
>
> Algorithm:
>
> 3/8 divided by 1/4 = 3/8 x 4/1
>
> 3/8 x 4/1 = 12/8
>
> 12/8 = 3/2
>
> 3/2 = 1 1/2

**15. One divided by a fraction**

Example: 1 divided by 2/3

> Story problem: A serving size is 2/3 of a candy bar. If I eat 1 whole candy bar, how many servings did I eat?
>
> Work:
>
> 1 divided by 2/3 means: How many groups of 2/3 fit into 1?
>
> One full serving uses 2/3 of the candy bar.
>
> There is 1/3 left.
>
> Since 1/3 is half of 2/3, the leftover amount is 1/2 of another serving.
>
> 1 divided by 2/3 = 1 1/2.
>
> Answer: 1 1/2.

**16. Common denominator method for division**

Example: 3/5 divided by 2/3

> First, rename both fractions with a common denominator. 3/5 = 9/15; 2/3 = 10/15
>
> Now divide: 9/15 divided by 10/15
>
> Since both fractions are in fifteenths, compare 9 pieces to 10 pieces: 9 divided by 10 = 9/10
>
> Answer: 3/5 divided by 2/3 = 9/10

Using the reciprocal method to check:

> 3/5 divided by 2/3 = 3/5 x 3/2
>
> 3/5 x 3/2 = 9/10

Both methods give the same answer.

**17. Why “invert and multiply” works**

“Invert and multiply” should not just be treated as a trick. It works because multiplying by the reciprocal changes the divisor into 1.

Example: 7/8 divided by 5/6

> Dividing by 5/6 is difficult, so we multiply by the reciprocal of 5/6, which is 6/5.
>
> 5/6 x 6/5 = 30/30; 30/30 = 1
>
> So: 7/8 divided by 5/6 becomes 7/8 x 6/5
>
> Then: 7/8 x 6/5 = 42/40; 42/40 = 21/20; 21/20 = 1 1/20

The shortcut works, but students should understand why it works instead of only memorizing the phrase “keep, change, flip.”
