<p><em>VdW Ch. 8</em></p>
<p><a href="https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1i6S0_S7Wi9dUNvoC-kwKO15M9jgVjr0Z6J7QQ9zx_UY/edit?slide=id.p1#slide=id.p1"><em><u>Class Slides 4</u></em></a></p></td>
<td><ol type="1">
<li><p>The six parts of number sense give teachers specific ways to check and grow students' math intuition. These six parts are: subitizing (instantly recognizing small quantities), flexible counting, comparing quantities (more or less), benchmark numbers (5 and 10), part whole relationships, and place value.</p></li>
<li><p>Part whole relationships are at the heart of number sense. They help students see that one number can be made from different combinations. For example, with place value, the idea that "10 is a bundle of ten ones" is the most important leap in understanding the base ten system. Students need to realize that 10 is both a group of ten ones and also a single counting unit (one "ten").</p></li>
<li><p>The "teacher hat" thinking during class activities, such as asking "Why would a teacher have students do this?" and "What math skills does it build?", is valuable training for learning how to design good lessons.</p></li>
</ol></td>

<td><p>Meanings of the Four Operations</p>
<p>(Story Problem Structures)</p>
<p><em>VdW Ch. 9</em></p>
<p><a href="https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1i6S0_S7Wi9dUNvoC-kwKO15M9jgVjr0Z6J7QQ9zx_UY/edit?slide=id.p1#slide=id.p1"><em><u>Class Slides 4</u></em></a></p></td>
<td><p>1. Having students "act out" and "model" word problems is a helpful way to build their understanding of what operations mean.</p>
<p>2. Addition and subtraction have four basic structures (join, separate, part whole, and compare), and the position of the unknown number in each structure greatly changes how hard the problem is. Multiplication and division also have four structures (equal groups, arrays, area, and comparison) that help students understand the different meanings of these operations. Division has two very different meanings: fair sharing (partition) and measurement (repeated subtraction).</p>
<p>3. Operations are connected to each other and should be taught as a connected whole. Teachers should teach addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division as related operations instead of waiting until students "master" one before moving to the next. In class, we emphasized that subtraction can be seen as a "missing addend problem" (for example, 10 minus 8 can be thought of as "what plus 8 equals 10"), and division can be seen as a "missing factor problem" (for example, 36 divided by 4 can be thought of as "4 times what equals 36").</p></td>
