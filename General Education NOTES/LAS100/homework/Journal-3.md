It's really important to be able to spot the signs of stress in yourself and your colleagues if you want to create a supportive environment. Mental health training courses say that common signs of stress include noticeable changes in mood, behaviour and physical health. For instance, a normally sociable colleague might become more reclusive, avoid socialising, seem disinterested in activities they once enjoyed and make negative comments. They might also show signs of irritability, persistent fatigue or difficulty concentrating. On a physical level, stress can show itself in headaches, changes in appetite or sleep disturbances.

To relieve stress and improve my mental health, I need to acknowledge my emotions. There are also a few strategies that I can use to help me cope better. Try to get some exercise every day, whether that's jogging, yoga or just a quick walk. I can also get into the habit of having a balanced daily routine that includes time for hobbies, relaxation and getting enough sleep. It's also a good idea to stay in touch with friends and family, and to ask for help if you need it. This can be a great way to get emotional support and a sense of belonging. Of course, one option is to seek help from a psychotherapist.

There are different views on whether it's a good idea to see a psychotherapist. From my perspective, seeking professional help at any time shows courage and knowledge. It demonstrates that you know how to take care of yourself and protect your mental health. It also shows respect for yourself. Therapists provide a professional, confidential space to discuss issues and develop coping strategies. This is very helpful for people who need mental health support.

UIUC has a great range of mental health services for students. The Counselling Centre offers individual and group counselling sessions, crisis intervention, and workshops on related topics such as stress management. The McKinley Health Centre offers psychiatric services, including assessment and medication management when necessary. There are also peer support programmes and student organisations that focus on mental health advocacy and education. These services are so comprehensive that students can use them without hesitation to protect their mental health.
