## Rank–Nullity

**E.g.** $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$, $T(x) = Ax$:

$$\underbrace{(\#\text{ of pivot col. of } A)}_{=\operatorname{rank}(T)} + \underbrace{(\#\text{ of free col. of } A)}_{=\operatorname{nullity}(T)} = n\ (= \dim\mathbb{R}^n)$$

*Proof.* $T: V \to W$, $\ker(T) \le V$. By the Complementation Theorem, $\ker(T) \oplus V_1 = V$ ($V_1 \le V$).

Consider $T|_{V_1}: V_1 \to T(V_1)\ (= \operatorname{Im}(T|_{V_1}))$, $T|_{V_1}(v_1) = T(v_1)$, i.e. $T|_{V_1}$ is the restricted linear trans. from $V_1$ to $T(V_1)$.

**Step ①** (WTS: $\dim(V_1) = \dim(T(V_1))$) $\Leftarrow$ WTS: $T|_{V_1}$ is an isomorphism of vector spaces.

1. $T|_{V_1}$ is surj. $\Leftarrow$ $T|_{V_1}: V_1 \to T(V_1)$ (如果到 $W$, 则不一定).
2. $T|_{V_1}$ is inj.: consider $v \in \ker(T|_{V_1}) \Rightarrow T|_{V_1}(v) = T(v) = \mathbf{0}_W$ and $v \in V_1$
   $\Rightarrow v \in V_1 \cap \ker(T) \Rightarrow v = \mathbf{0}_V \Rightarrow \ker(T|_{V_1}) = \{\mathbf{0}\}$.

**Step ②** (WTS: $\dim(T(V_1)) = \dim(\operatorname{Im}(T)) = \dim(T(V))$, $T: V \to W$)
$\Leftarrow$ WTS: $\dim \operatorname{Im}(V_1) \le \dim\operatorname{Im}(V)$ and the reverse $\Leftarrow$ $T(V_1) \supseteq T(V)$.

Take $\forall\, T(v) = T(v_k + v') \in \operatorname{Im}(V)$ with $v_k \in \ker(T)$, $v' \in V_1$ ($\ker(T) \oplus V_1 = V$)
$\Rightarrow T(v) = T(v_k + v') = \mathbf{0} + T(v')$ (l. trans.) $\Rightarrow T(v) \in T(V_1)$
$\Rightarrow T(V) \le T(V_1) \Rightarrow \dim T(V_1) = \dim T(V) = \dim(\operatorname{Im}(T))$.

Hence $\dim V = \dim\ker T + \dim V_1 = n(T) + \operatorname{rank}(T)$ ∎

## Coordinate Vectors & Matrix Representations

(Goal: express a vector $v \in V$ by a column vector $[v]_B \in \mathbb{R}^n$.)

### Coordinate vectors

**Def.** $V$: a finite-dimensional vector space; $B = \{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$: an **ordered** basis of $V$. For $v = \alpha_1 v_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n v_n$, $[v]_B := (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)^T$.

**E.g.** $V = \mathbb{R}^2$, $B = \{(1,0)^T, (0,1)^T\}$, $v = (2,1)^T$: $v = 2(1,0)^T + 1(0,1)^T \Rightarrow [v]_B = (2,1)^T$.
$B' = \{(1,1)^T, (1,-1)^T\}$: $v = \frac32(1,1)^T + \frac12(1,-1)^T \Rightarrow [v]_{B'} = (\frac32, \frac12)^T$.

**Remark.** Fix any $v \in V$ and ordered basis $B$; the coordinate vector $[v]_B$ is **uniquely determined**.
*Proof.* $v = \alpha_1 v_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n v_n = \beta_1 v_1 + \cdots + \beta_n v_n \Rightarrow (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)^T = (\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n)^T$ (unique expression).

**Theorem.** Let $V$ and $B$ be as above. Then the function $[\ \cdot\ ]_B: V \to \mathbb{F}^n$, $v \mapsto [v]_B$ is ① well-defined and ② an isomorphism.

(In set theory, a function $f: P \to Q$ is well-defined $\iff$ $f(p)$ takes only one value and $f(p) \in Q$. e.g. a "function" $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ sending $4 \mapsto 2$ and $4 \mapsto -2$ is not well-defined.)

*Proof ①.* ($\Leftarrow$ Remark.)
*Proof ②.*
1. WTS: $[\ ]_B: V \to \mathbb{F}^n$ is a linear trans. $\iff [v + w]_B = [v]_B + [w]_B$ (and scalars).
2. WTS: $[\ ]_B$ is surj.: $\forall (\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_n)^T \in \mathbb{F}^n$, let $v' := \gamma_1 v_1 + \cdots + \gamma_n v_n \Rightarrow [v']_B = (\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_n)^T$.
3. WTS: $[\ ]_B$ is inj. $\iff ([v]_B = [v']_B \Rightarrow v = v')$.

## Change of Basis Matrix

**Def.** $B = \{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$ and $B' = \{w_1,\dots,w_n\}$ are 2 ordered bases of $V$, with

$$v_1 = \alpha_{11}w_1 + \cdots + \alpha_{n1}w_n,\quad\dots,\quad v_n = \alpha_{1n}w_1 + \cdots + \alpha_{nn}w_n$$

Change of basis matrix: $\mathcal{C}_{B'B} = (\alpha_{ij})$, where $v_j = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{ij} w_i$.

**E.g.** $V = P_2(\mathbb{R})$, $B = \{1 + x + x^2,\ 2x,\ 5x + 3x^2\}$, $B' = \{1, x, x^2\}$. Then

$$\mathcal{C}_{B'B} = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 & 0\\ 1 & 2 & 5\\ 1 & 0 & 3\end{pmatrix}\quad(\text{注意方向、顺序!})$$

**Prop.** The coordinate vectors $[v]_{B'}$ and $[v]_B$ corresponding to $B'$, $B$ are related by $\mathcal{C}_{B'B}[v]_B = [v]_{B'}$.
