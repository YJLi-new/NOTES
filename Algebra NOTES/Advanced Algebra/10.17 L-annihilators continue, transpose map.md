## Annihilator vs. dual of the quotient

$\operatorname{Ann}(W) \le V^*$ ($W \le V$), $\dim(V) < \infty$:

$\dim(\operatorname{Ann}(W)) = \dim(V/W) = k$, with bases $\{f_1, f_2, \dots, f_k\}$ of $\operatorname{Ann}(W)$ and $\{v_1 + W, \dots, v_k + W\}$ of $V/W$.

Then $\phi: \operatorname{Ann}(W) \xrightarrow{\cong} V/W$ are isomorphic, where $\phi(f_i) := v_i + W$, $\forall i = 1,\dots,k$.

Note: the definition of $\phi$ involves a basis of $\operatorname{Ann}(W)$ & a basis of $V/W$.
WT: define $\Phi: \operatorname{Ann}(W) \xrightarrow{\cong} (V/W)^*$ **without** using any choice of bases.

**Def.**

$$\Phi: \operatorname{Ann}(W) \to (V/W)^*,\quad f \mapsto \bar f,\qquad \text{where } \bar f(v + W) = f(v)\quad (f \in \operatorname{Ann}(W) \le V^*)$$

($f: V \to \mathbb{F}$ in $\operatorname{Ann}(W)$ satisfies $W \le \ker(f)$ ★ $\Rightarrow \bar f: V/W \to \mathbb{F}$ is well-defined — quotient space note.)

**Prop 1.** $\Phi$ is a natural linear transformation, i.e. $\Phi(af + bg) = a\Phi(f) + b\Phi(g)$.

*Proof.* (WTS: $\Phi(f + g) = \cdots : V/W \to \mathbb{F}$ $\to$ WTS: $\Phi(f + g)(v + W) = (\Phi(f) + \Phi(g))(v + W)$, $\forall v + W \in V/W$.)
LHS: $\Phi(f + g)(v + W) = \overline{(f + g)}(v + W) = (f + g)(v) = f(v) + g(v)$.
RHS: $(\Phi(f) + \Phi(g))(v + W) = (\bar f + \bar g)(v + W) = \bar f(v + W) + \bar g(v + W) = f(v) + g(v)$.
$\Rightarrow$ LHS = RHS.

**Prop 2.** $\Phi: \operatorname{Ann}(W) \to (V/W)^*$ is injective.

*Proof.* Suppose $f \in \ker(\Phi)$ (recall: $f: V \to \mathbb{F}$ is in $\operatorname{Ann}(W)$). Then $\Phi(f) = \mathbf{0}_{(V/W)^*}: V/W \to \mathbb{F}$, i.e. $\Phi(f)(v + W) = \mathbf{0}_{(V/W)^*}(v + W)$, $\forall v + W \in V/W$
$\Rightarrow \bar f(v + W) = 0_{\mathbb{F}} \Rightarrow f(v) = 0,\ \forall v \in V \Rightarrow f = \mathbf{0}_{V^*} = \mathbf{0}_{\operatorname{Ann}(W)}$.

**Corollary.** If $\dim(V) < \infty$, then $\Phi: \operatorname{Ann}(W) \xrightarrow{\cong} (V/W)^*$ is a natural isomorphism.

*Proof.* 已知道: (1) $\dim(\operatorname{Ann}(W)) = \dim((V/W)^*)$; (2) (Prop. 2) $\Phi$ is inj. $\Rightarrow \Phi$ is bij.

**Remark.** The Corollary lies on (1) $\dim(V) = \dim(W) < \infty$ and (2) $T: V \to W$ is inj. $\Rightarrow T: V \xrightarrow{\cong} W$. This fails if $\dim(V) = \infty$ ★. 但仍有 $\Phi: \operatorname{Ann}(W) \cong (V/W)^*$ for $\infty$-dim vector spaces — need the transpose map.

## Transpose Map

**Def.** $T: V \to W$. The **transpose** of $T$ is $T^*: W^* \to V^*$, $f \mapsto T^*(f)$, s.t. $\forall f \in W^*$, $\forall v \in V$:

$$[T^*(f)](v) := f(\underbrace{T(v)}_{\in W}) \in \mathbb{F}$$

**Remark.**

1. By def., $T^*(f): V \to \mathbb{F}$ is a map. But $T^*(f) = f \circ T \in V^*$ is a linear functional. (不同? ★)
2. By (1), $T^*$ does map $W^*$ to $V^*$. In fact, $T^*: W^* \to V^*$ is a linear trans.
   e.g. WTS: $T^*(f + g) = T^*(f) + T^*(g)$ ($\in V^*$; $f, g \in W^*$) $\to$ WTS: $T^*(f + g)(v) = (T^*(f) + T^*(g))(v)$, $\forall v \in V$.
   *Proof.* RHS $= T^*(f)(v) + T^*(g)(v) = f(T(v)) + g(T(v)) = (f + g)(T(v)) = $ LHS.

**Prop 1.** $T: V \to W$.

1. $T$ is inj. $\Rightarrow T^*$ is surj.
2. $T$ is surj. $\Rightarrow T^*$ is inj.

*Proof of 2.* Suppose $T: V \to W$ is surj. $\Rightarrow T^*: W^* \to V^*$, and suppose $f \in \ker(T^*)$
$\Rightarrow T^*(f) = \mathbf{0}_{V^*} \Rightarrow T^*(f)(v) = \mathbf{0}_{V^*}(v),\ \forall v \in V \Rightarrow f(T(v)) = 0$
$\overset{T\text{ surj.}}{\Rightarrow} f(w) = 0,\ \forall w = T(v) \in W \Rightarrow f = \mathbf{0}_{W^*}$ ★

**Prop 2** (见图: Prop. 5.6). $(T_{BA})^t = T^*_{A^*B^*}$, where $T: V_A \to W_B$, $T^*: W^*_{B^*} \to V^*_{A^*}$. *Proof:* 见本周 Tut.

## Inverse of $\Phi$ via the transpose

Recall: $\Phi: \operatorname{Ann}(W) \to (V/W)^*$.
Recall: for $W \le V$, the "canonical projection" $\pi_W: V \to V/W$, $\pi_W(v) = v + W$ ($\ker\pi_W = W$).

Construct the inverse of $\Phi$ using the transpose. Consider $(\pi_W)^*: (V/W)^* \to V^*$. Then $\forall f \in (V/W)^*$, $(\pi_W)^*(f) \in V^*$ is given by

$$(\pi_W)^*(f)(v) := f(\pi_W(v)) = f(v + W),\quad \forall v \in V$$

Then $\forall w \in W$: $(\pi_W)^*(f)(w) = f(w + W) = f(\mathbf{0} + W) = f(\mathbf{0}_{V/W}) = 0$
$\Rightarrow (\pi_W)^*(f) \in \operatorname{Ann}(W) \Rightarrow (\pi_W)^*: (V/W)^* \to \operatorname{Ann}(W)$, and this is the inverse of $\Phi$.
