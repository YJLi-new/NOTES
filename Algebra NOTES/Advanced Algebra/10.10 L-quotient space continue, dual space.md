## Quotient Space

**Prop. 1.** $\pi_W: V \to V/W$, $\pi_W(v) := v + W$. Then $\pi_W$ is (1) linear & (2) surj. & (3) $\ker(\pi_W) = W$.

*Proof.*
1. $\pi_W(v + v') = (v + v') + W = (v + W) + (v' + W)$ (vector space addition in $V/W$) $= \pi_W(v) + \pi_W(v')$. Ex.: 证 $\pi_W(\alpha v) = \alpha\pi_W(v)$.
2. Obviously.
3. $\pi_W(v) = \mathbf{0}_{V/W} = \mathbf{0} + W \iff v + W = \mathbf{0} + W \overset{\text{Theorem}}{\iff} v - \mathbf{0} \in W$ ★

**Prop. 2.** If $\dim(V) < \infty$ and $W \le V$, then $\dim(V/W) = \dim(V) - \dim(W)$.

*Proof.* Apply the rank–nullity theorem on $\pi_W$:
$\dim(V) = \dim(\underbrace{\ker\pi_W}_{=W}) + \dim(\underbrace{\operatorname{Im}\pi_W}_{=V/W})$.

## Induced map $\bar T$

**Def.** $T: V \to W$ (old l. trans.). Suppose $V' \le \ker(T)$. Define the new l. trans.

$$\bar T: V/V' \to W,\qquad \bar T(v + V') := T(v)$$

**Prop. 3.**

1. $\bar T$ is well-defined, i.e. suppose $v + V' = u + V'$ ($v \ne u$); then (WTS) $\bar T(v + V') = \bar T(u + V') \iff T(v) = T(u)$.
   *Proof.* $T(v) - T(u) = T(v - u)$, and $v - u \in V'$, so $= T(v')$ with $v' \in V'$; $= \mathbf{0}$ (∵ $V' \le \ker T$) $\Rightarrow T(v) = T(u)$.
2. $\bar T$ is a linear transformation.
   *Proof.* $\bar T((v + V') + (u + V')) = \bar T((v + u) + V') = T(v + u) = T(v) + T(u) = \bar T(v + V') + \bar T(u + V')$ (def. of $\bar T$).

(HW: 证 if we take $V' = \ker(T)$, then $\bar T: V/\ker(T) \to W$ is injective.)

## Dual Space $V^*$

**Def.** $V^* := \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(V, \mathbb{F})$ ($\mathbb{F}$: a 1-dim vector space). All elements $\phi$ in $V^*$ are called **linear functionals**.

**Recall.**

1. $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(V, W)$ is a vector space (见 HW4). Suppose $\phi, \psi \in \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(V, W)$; then $\phi + \psi$, $\alpha\phi \in \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(V, W)$ are defined by $(\phi + \psi)(v) := \phi(v) + \psi(v)$, $(\alpha\phi)(v) := \alpha\phi(v)$.
2. $\forall$ l. trans. $\phi: V \to W$, if $B := \{v_i \mid i \in I\}$ is a basis of $V$, then $\phi$ is uniquely determined by the values $\phi(v_i)$ for all $i \in I$.
   e.g. for $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$, if we know $T(e_i)$ for $i = 1,\dots,n$, then $T(v) = T\left(\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i e_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i T(e_i)$.

**E.g.**

1. $V = M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F})$; define $\phi: V \to \mathbb{F}$ by $\phi(A) := A_{11}$. Claim: $\phi \in V^* = \operatorname{Hom}(V, \mathbb{F})$ (见 …).
2. $V = M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F})$, $\operatorname{tr}: V \to \mathbb{F}$ (trace), $\operatorname{tr}(A) := A_{11} + A_{22} + \cdots + A_{nn}$. Then $\operatorname{tr} \in V^*$.
3. $V = M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F})$, $\det: V \to \mathbb{F}$. Then for $n > 1$, $\det \notin V^*$, since $\det(A + B) \ne \det(A) + \det(B)$ in general. Also $\det(\alpha A) = \alpha^n\det(A) \ne \alpha\det(A)$.
4. $V = C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, $\phi: V \to \mathbb{F}$, $\phi(f) := f''(1)$. Then $\phi \in V^*$, e.g. $\phi(f + g) = (f + g)''(1) = f''(1) + g''(1) = \phi(f) + \phi(g)$.

**Def.** Let $B = \{v_i \mid i \in I\}$ be a basis of $V$ ($I$ can be infinite). For each $i \in I$ define $\phi_i: V \to \mathbb{F}$ by:

- Step 1 (on $B$): $\phi_i(v_j) = \delta_{ij} := \begin{cases}1 & i = j\\ 0 & i \ne j\end{cases}$
- Step 2 (on $V$): for any $v = \alpha_1 v_{i_1} + \cdots + \alpha_l v_{i_l} \in V$ ($v_{i_k} \in B$), $\phi_i(v) := \sum_k \alpha_k \phi_i(v_{i_k})$.

(Ex.) Then by construction $\phi_i \in V^*$. Define $B^* = \{\phi_i \mid i \in I\} \subseteq V^*$.
