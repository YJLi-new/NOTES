## Tensor product $V\otimes W$ ($= \mathcal{X}/\mathcal{Y}$)

(见 … previous lecture) $(av_1 + bv_2)\otimes w = a(v_1\otimes w) + b(v_2\otimes w)$.

**Theorem (Universal Property, not examinable).** $f: V\times W \to U$ is a 2-linear map ⇒ ∃ $\Phi: V\otimes W \to U$ s.t. ① $\Phi$ is a linear trans. and ② $\Phi(v\otimes w) = f(v, w)$. (Study $\Phi$ → understand $f$.)

$i(v, w) := v\otimes w$ is an injective map, and $f = \Phi\circ i$:

$$V\times W \xrightarrow{\ i\ } V\otimes W \xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ } U$$

*Proof.* Consider $\Pi: \mathcal{X} \to U$ ($\mathcal{X} = \operatorname{Span}(\mathcal{S})$),

$$\Pi\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\{v_i, w_i\}\Big) := \sum_{i=1}^n a_if(v_i, w_i) \in U,\quad \forall a_i \in \mathbb{F}$$

Note: ① $\Pi$ is a linear trans.; ② $\{v + v', w\} - \{v, w\} - \{v', w\} \in \ker(\Pi)$ (见 …); ③ (similarly for the other generators) $\mathcal{Y} \le \ker(\Pi)$.
By ③ and a theorem about quotient spaces we have $\pi: \mathcal{X}/\mathcal{Y} \to U$, $\pi(\{v, w\} + \mathcal{Y}) := \Pi(\{v, w\}) = f(v, w)$, and this $\pi = \Phi$.

**Theorem 2.** (见 …: if $\{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$ is a basis of $V$ and $\{w_1,\dots,w_m\}$ is a basis of $W$, then $\mathcal{C} = \{v_i\otimes w_j\}$ is a basis of $V\otimes W$.)

*Proof (not examinable).*
① A general element in $V\otimes W$ is of the form $v_1\otimes w_1 + \cdots + v_k\otimes w_k$ ⇒ $\mathcal{C}$ spans $V\otimes W$.
② (WTS: $\mathcal{C}$ is l.i.) Consider $\sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{i=1}^n\gamma_{ij}(v_i\otimes w_j) = \mathbf{0}$ $(**)$. (WTS: $\forall i, j$, $\gamma_{ij} = 0$.)
Take the dual bases $A^* = \{\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n\}$ of $V^*$ and $B^* = \{\psi_1,\dots,\psi_m\}$ of $W^*$. Let $\varepsilon(v, w) := \phi_i(v)\psi_j(w)$ (2-linear; e.g. $\varepsilon(kv, w) = \phi_i(kv)\psi_j(w) = k\phi_i(v)\psi_j(w) = k\varepsilon(v, w)$).
By the universal property, ∃ l. trans. $E: V\otimes W \to \mathbb{F}$ s.t. $E(v\otimes w) = \varepsilon(v, w) = \phi_i(v)\psi_j(w)$.
Apply $E$ to $(**)$ … (见 …) ⇒ $\gamma_{ij} = 0$.

**Theorem 3.** $T: V \to V'$ and $S: W \to W'$ are linear trans. ⇒ ∃ a linear trans. $T\otimes S: V\otimes W \to V'\otimes W'$ satisfying $T\otimes S(v\otimes w) = T(v)\otimes S(w)$.

*Proof.* Consider $\delta: V\times W \to V'\otimes W'$, $\delta(v, w) := T(v)\otimes S(w)$ — a 2-linear map; apply the universal property. (e.g. 见 …)

**E.g.** $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$, $T(z) = \begin{pmatrix}a & b\\ c & d\end{pmatrix}z$ (as drawn), $S: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$, $S(y) = \begin{pmatrix}p & q & r\\ s & t & u\\ v & w & x\end{pmatrix}y$ (as drawn); $A = \{e_1, e_2\}$, $B = \{f_1, f_2, f_3\}$ usual bases. $T\otimes S: \mathbb{R}^2\otimes\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2\otimes\mathbb{R}^3$. By Thm 2, a basis of $\mathbb{R}^2\otimes\mathbb{R}^3$ is $\mathcal{C} = \{e_1\otimes f_1, \dots\}$ (6 elements).

(Whiteboard) Let's compute $(T\otimes S)_{\mathcal{CC}}$:
$(T\otimes S)(e_1\otimes f_1) = T(e_1)\otimes S(f_1) = \begin{pmatrix}a\\c\end{pmatrix}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}p\\s\\v\end{pmatrix} = ap(e_1\otimes f_1) + as(e_1\otimes f_2) + av(e_1\otimes f_3) + cp(e_2\otimes f_1) + cs(e_2\otimes f_2) + cv(e_2\otimes f_3)$ — the 1st column; similarly for $e_1\otimes f_2, \dots, e_2\otimes f_3$. Hence

$$(T\otimes S)_{\mathcal{CC}} = \begin{pmatrix}a\,S_{BB} & b\,S_{BB}\\ c\,S_{BB} & d\,S_{BB}\end{pmatrix}\quad(\text{Kronecker product})$$

## Summary on tensor products (whiteboard)

| | $V\otimes W$ | $V_1\otimes V_2\otimes\cdots\otimes V_m$ |
|---|---|---|
| $m$-linearity | $v\otimes(aw + bw') = a(v\otimes w) + b(v\otimes w')$ | $v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes(av_i + bv_i')\otimes\cdots\otimes v_m = a(\cdots\otimes v_i\otimes\cdots) + b(\cdots\otimes v_i'\otimes\cdots)$ |
| Universal property | $f: V\times W \to U$ bilinear ⇝ $\Phi: V\otimes W \to U$ linear | $f: V_1\times\cdots\times V_m \to U$ $m$-linear ⇝ $\Phi: V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_m \to U$ linear |
| General element | $v^{(1)}\otimes w^{(1)} + \cdots + v^{(k)}\otimes w^{(k)}$ | $v_1^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_m^{(1)} + \cdots + v_1^{(k)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_m^{(k)}$ |
| Dimension | $\dim(V)\cdot\dim(W)$ | $\dim(V_1)\cdot\dim(V_2)\cdots\dim(V_m)$ |
| Linear trans. | $T: V \to V'$, $S: W \to W'$ ⇝ $T\otimes S: V\otimes W \to V'\otimes W'$ | $T_i: V_i \to V_i'$ ⇝ $T_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_m$ with $T_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_m(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_m) = T_1(v_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes T_m(v_m)$ |
