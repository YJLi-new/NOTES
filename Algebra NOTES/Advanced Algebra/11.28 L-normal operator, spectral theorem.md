## Normal operators

**Def.** $(V, \langle\,,\rangle)$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-inner product space. $T: V \to V$ is **normal** $\iff T^*T = TT^*$.

- **E.g. 1.** $T$ is self-adjoint ⇒ $T^* = T$ ⇒ $T^*T = T^2 = TT^*$ ⇒ $T$ is normal.
- **E.g. 2.** $T$ is unitary ⇒ $T^*T = I$; with $\dim(V) < \infty$ ⇒ $T^{-1} = T^*$ ⇒ $TT^* = I$ ⇒ $T^*T = I = TT^*$.

**Remark.**
- $\dim(V) = \infty$: $T$ is unitary $\iff T^*T = TT^* = I$.
- $\dim(V) < \infty$: $T$ is unitary $\iff T^*T = I \Rightarrow TT^* = I$.

Hence $T$ is unitary ⇒ $\forall\dim(V)$, $T$ is normal.

**Theorem.** $(V, \langle\,,\rangle)$ is finite-dimensional and $T: V \to V$ is normal ⇒ ∃ orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of $T$.

**Prop.** $T$ is normal ⇒ (1)–(4) (见书):

1. $\|T(v)\| = \|T^*(v)\|$.
   *Proof.* $\|T(v)\|^2 = \langle T(v), T(v)\rangle = \langle T^*T(v), v\rangle \overset{T\text{ normal}}{=} \langle TT^*(v), v\rangle = \langle v, TT^*(v)\rangle = \langle T^*(v), T^*(v)\rangle = \|T^*(v)\|^2$.
2. $T - \lambda I$ is normal:
   $$\begin{aligned}(T - \lambda I)^*(T - \lambda I) &= (T^* - \bar\lambda I)(T - \lambda I) = T^*T - \lambda T^* - \bar\lambda T + \bar\lambda\lambda I\\ &\overset{T\text{ normal}}{=} TT^* - \lambda T^* - \bar\lambda T + \bar\lambda\lambda I = (T - \lambda I)(T^* - \bar\lambda I) = (T - \lambda I)(T - \lambda I)^*\end{aligned}$$
3. $T(v) = \lambda v \iff T^*(v) = \bar\lambda v$:
   $T(v) = \lambda v \iff (T - \lambda I)v = \mathbf{0} \iff \|(T - \lambda I)v\|^2 = 0 \overset{(T - \lambda I)\text{ normal}, (1)}{\iff} \|(T - \lambda I)^*v\|^2 = 0 \iff \|(T^* - \bar\lambda I)v\|^2 = 0 \iff (T^* - \bar\lambda I)v = \mathbf{0}$ ★
4. Eigenvectors with distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal. (Whiteboard) If $T(v) = \lambda v$, $T(w) = \mu w$:
   $\langle T^*(v), w\rangle = \langle v, T(w)\rangle = \langle v, \mu w\rangle = \mu\langle v, w\rangle$, and by (3) $\langle T^*(v), w\rangle = \langle\bar\lambda v, w\rangle = \lambda\langle v, w\rangle$.
   ∴ $\lambda\langle v, w\rangle = \mu\langle v, w\rangle$ ⇒ $(\lambda - \mu)\langle v, w\rangle = 0$, $\lambda - \mu \ne 0$ ⇒ $\langle v, w\rangle = 0$.

### Proof of the Theorem

Use induction on $\dim V$.

- When $\dim(V) = 1$: take any unit vector in $V$ as a basis of $V$.
- Assume that for $\forall$ vector space $V_k$ with $\dim(V_k) = k$ and $\forall$ normal operator $T_k: V_k \to V_k$, the theorem is true.
- Assume $V_{k+1}$ is a vector space with $\dim(V_{k+1}) = k + 1$ and $T_{k+1}: V_{k+1} \to V_{k+1}$ is a normal operator. (WTS: ∃ orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of $T_{k+1}$ in $V_{k+1}$.)

**Claim 1.** $T_{k+1}$ has an eigenvector $u$ (∃; ⇒ every vector of $U$ is an eigenvector of $T_{k+1}$). Then take $U := \operatorname{Span}\{u\}$ ($\dim U = 1$) and consider $U^\perp$ ($\dim U^\perp = k$) s.t. $V = U \oplus U^\perp$.

**Claim 2.** $U^\perp \le V$ is $T$-invariant.
*Proof.* Take $\forall w \in U^\perp$. Then $\forall u' \in U$: $\langle u', T(w)\rangle = \langle T^*(u'), w\rangle \overset{\text{Prop. (3)}}{=} \langle\bar\lambda u', w\rangle = \lambda\langle u', w\rangle = 0$ ⇒ $T(w) \in U^\perp$.

## Corollary (Spectral Theorem)

$(V, \langle\,,\rangle)$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-inner product space, $T: V \to V$ is normal ⇒ ∃ operators $P_1,\dots,P_k$ s.t.

- (a) $\forall i$, $P_i$ is self-adjoint ($P_i^* = P_i$);
- (b) $\forall i$, $P_i$ is idempotent ($P_i^2 = P_i$);
- (c) $\forall i \ne j$, $P_iP_j = 0$;
- (d) $\sum_{i=1}^k P_i = P_1 + \cdots + P_k = I$, and $T = \lambda_1P_1 + \cdots + \lambda_kP_k$, where the $\lambda_i$'s are the eigenvalues of $T$.

*Proof.* $T$ is normal ⇒ (previous Thm) $T$ has a basis of eigenvectors ⇒ $T$ is diagonalizable ⇒ $m_T(x) = (x - \lambda_1)\cdots(x - \lambda_k)$, where the $\lambda_i$'s are the eigenvalues of $T$ and are **distinct**.

Recall the proof of PDT ($\forall i$, $V_i = \ker(T - \lambda_iI)$ ⇒ $V = V_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_k$). Let

$$q_i(x) = \frac{m_T(x)}{x - \lambda_i} = (x - \lambda_1)\cdots(x - \lambda_{i-1})(x - \lambda_{i+1})\cdots(x - \lambda_k)$$

⇒ $\gcd(q_1(x),\dots,q_k(x)) = 1$ ⇒ (Bézout) ∃ $a_1(x),\dots,a_k(x)$ s.t. $a_1(x)q_1(x) + \cdots + a_k(x)q_k(x) = 1$.

Take $P_i := a_i(T)q_i(T)$; then $P_1 + \cdots + P_k = I$ (→ (d)), i.e. $P_1(v) + \cdots + P_k(v) = v$ with $P_i(v) \in V_i$.

(Whiteboard) If $i \ne j$, then $P_iP_j = a_i(T)q_i(T)a_j(T)q_j(T) = a_i(T)a_j(T)q_i(T)q_j(T) = a_i(T)a_j(T)\cdots m_T(T) = 0$ (→ (c)).

$P_i = P_iI \overset{(d)}{=} P_i(P_1 + \cdots + P_k) \overset{(c)}{=} P_iP_i = P_i^2$ (→ (b)).

$\forall v \in V$:
$T(v) = T\circ I(v) = T(P_1 + \cdots + P_k)(v) = T(P_1(v)) + \cdots + T(P_k(v))$, with $P_i(v) \in V_i = \ker(T - \lambda_iI)$, i.e. $(T - \lambda_iI)(P_i(v)) = T(P_i(v)) - \lambda_iP_i(v) = \mathbf{0}$ ⇒ $T(P_i(v)) = \lambda_iP_i(v)$
$= \lambda_1P_1(v) + \cdots + \lambda_kP_k(v)$, i.e. $T(v) = (\lambda_1P_1 + \cdots + \lambda_kP_k)(v)$ $\forall v \in V$ (→ (d)).

(Whiteboard) So we're left with (a).
**Lemma.** $T: V \to V$ is normal $\iff T^* = p(T)$ for some polynomial $p(x) \in \mathbb{C}[x]$.
Assuming the Lemma, then $P_i := a_i(T)q_i(T) = b_nT^n + \cdots + b_1T + b_0I$, so $P_i^* = \bar b_n(T^*)^n + \cdots + \bar b_1T^* + \bar b_0I$. (续 …)
