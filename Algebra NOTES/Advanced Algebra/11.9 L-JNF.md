## Jordan Normal Form

$T: V \to V$, $m_T(x) = (x - \lambda_1)^{e_1}\cdots(x - \lambda_k)^{e_k}$
$\iff \exists$ a basis $A$ of $V$ s.t.

$$(T)_{AA} = \begin{pmatrix}J_1 & & 0\\ & J_2 & \\ & & \ddots\\ 0 & & & J_r\end{pmatrix}\ (\text{blocks}),\qquad \text{where } \forall i,\ J_i = \begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{l_i} & 1 & & 0\\ & \ddots & \ddots & \\ & & \ddots & 1\\ 0 & & & \lambda_{l_i}\end{pmatrix}$$

**Remarks.**

1. 可能 ∃ some $J_i$ and $J_p$ (two different Jordan blocks) s.t. $\lambda_{l_i} = \lambda_{l_p}$, but the diagonal entries $\lambda_{l_i}$ must $\in \{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k\}$.
2. For each $\lambda_p \in \{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_p,\dots,\lambda_k\}$, ∃ at least 1 Jordan block $J_i$ s.t. its diagonal entry $\lambda_{l_i} = \lambda_p$.
3. More precisely, let $J_{p_1},\dots,J_{p_\alpha}$ be the blocks in $(T)_{AA}$ with diagonal entry $\lambda_p$. Then the sizes of $J_{p_1},\dots,J_{p_\alpha}$ are **at most** $e_p\times e_p$ (and at least one has size exactly $e_p$).
   e.g. if $m_T(x) = (x - 5)^3(x - 4)^2$, then $T_{AA}$ cannot have a $5\times 5$ block with diagonal 5 (this is $5\times 5 > 3\times 3$).
4. In particular, if $\forall i$, $e_i = 1$, then $\forall i$, $J_i$ must be a $1\times 1$ block. Then $T_{AA} = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k)$ is diagonal.
5. By changing the order of the basis $A$, we can "move" the $J_i$'s in $(T)_{AA}$.
   Note that (section on matrix representations), letting $C = \mathcal{C}_{AA'}$, $(T)_{A'A'} = C^{-1}(T)_{AA}C$ ⇒ the two block arrangements are similar matrices.

**E.g.** Let $T: \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^4$, $\chi_T(x) = x^4$. C.-H. $\Rightarrow m_T(x) = x$ or $x^2$ or $x^3$ or $x^4$. The possibilities of Jordan blocks (all with 0 on the diagonal):

| $m_T(x)$ | Jordan structure |
|---|---|
| $x^4$ | one $4\times 4$ block |
| $x^3$ | a $3\times 3$ block + a $1\times 1$ block |
| $x^2$ | a $2\times 2$ block + … (e.g. two $2\times 2$ blocks, or $2+1+1$) |
| $x$ | four $1\times 1$ blocks (zero matrix) |

## Proof of JNF

(3 steps; here the case (1) $m_T(x) = x^m$.)

(1) (HW7) $0 \subsetneq \ker(T) \subsetneq \ker(T^2) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \ker(T^m) = V$.
Denote $\ker(T^i) = K_i$ ($K_0 = 0$). Define $W_m = K_m/K_{m-1}$.
Let $\mathcal{B}_m' = \{u_1^m + K_{m-1},\dots,u_i^m + K_{m-1}\}$ be a basis of $W_m$.

**Claim 1.** $\mathcal{C}_m' := \{T(u_1^m) + K_{m-2},\dots,T(u_i^m) + K_{m-2}\} \subseteq V/K_{m-2}$ satisfies $\mathcal{C}_m' \subseteq W_{m-1} = K_{m-1}/K_{m-2}$. *Proof:* (见 …)

**Claim 2.** $\mathcal{C}_m'$ is l.i. in $W_{m-1}$.
*Proof.* Let $\alpha_1(T(u_1^m) + K_{m-2}) + \cdots + \alpha_i(T(u_i^m) + K_{m-2}) = \mathbf{0}_{V/K_{m-2}}$
$\iff T(\alpha_1u_1^m + \cdots + \alpha_iu_i^m) + K_{m-2} = \mathbf{0} + K_{m-2}$
$\iff T(\alpha_1u_1^m + \cdots + \alpha_iu_i^m) \in K_{m-2} = \ker(T^{m-2})$
$\iff T^{m-2}(T(\alpha_1u_1^m + \cdots + \alpha_iu_i^m)) = \mathbf{0}$
$\iff \alpha_1u_1^m + \cdots + \alpha_iu_i^m \in \ker(T^{m-1}) = K_{m-1}$
$\iff (\alpha_1u_1^m + \cdots + \alpha_iu_i^m) + K_{m-1} = \mathbf{0} + K_{m-1}$
$\iff \alpha_1(u_1^m + K_{m-1}) + \cdots + \alpha_i(u_i^m + K_{m-1}) = \mathbf{0}_{W_m}$
$\iff \alpha_1 = \cdots = \alpha_i = 0$ (since $\mathcal{B}_m'$ is a basis of $W_m$).

(Whiteboard summary: by HW7, $0 \subsetneq K_1 \subsetneq K_2 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq K_{m-1} \subsetneq K_m = V$ with $W_j = K_j/K_{j-1}$. In $W_{m-1}$ the l.i. set $\{T(u_1^m) + K_{m-2},\dots,T(u_i^m) + K_{m-2}\}$ is extended to a basis by adding $\{v_1 + K_{m-2},\dots,v_j + K_{m-2}\}$; then in $W_{m-2}$ one has $T^2(u^m)$'s, $T(v)$'s, extended by $w$'s; and so on down to $W_1$.)

**Claim 3.** (By HW7) the following forms a basis of $V$:

$$A = \left\{\begin{aligned}
&T^{m-1}(u_1^m),\dots,T^2(u_1^m),T(u_1^m),u_1^m,\\
&\quad\vdots\\
&T^{m-1}(u_i^m),\dots,T^2(u_i^m),T(u_i^m),u_i^m,\\
&T^{m-2}(v_1^m),\dots,T^2(v_1^m),T(v_1^m),v_1^m,\\
&\quad\vdots\\
&T^{m-2}(v_j^m),\dots,T(v_j^m),v_j^m,\\
&T^{m-3}(w_1^m),\dots,T^2(w_1^m),T(w_1^m),w_1^m,\\
&\quad\vdots
\end{aligned}\right\}$$

Then $(T)_{AA}$ is given by: $T(T^{m-1}(u_1^m)) = \mathbf{0}$, $T(T^{m-2}(u_1^m)) = 1\cdot T^{m-1}(u_1^m)$, …, $T(u_1^m) = 1\cdot T(u_1^m)$, …
$\Rightarrow T_{AA}$ is block diagonal with $m\times m$ Jordan blocks (one per $u^m$), then $(m-1)\times(m-1)$ blocks (one per $v$), then $(m-2)\times(m-2)$ blocks, …, all with 0 on the diagonal and 1 on the superdiagonal.
