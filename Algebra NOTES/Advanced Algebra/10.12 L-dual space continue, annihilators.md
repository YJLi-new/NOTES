## Dual space — remarks

1. The "zero vector" in $V^* = \operatorname{Hom}(V, \mathbb{F})$ is $\mathbf{0}_{V^*}: V \to \mathbb{F}$, where $\forall v \in V$, $\mathbf{0}_{V^*}(v) = 0$ (为标量 0).
2. In general, $\forall \phi \in \operatorname{Hom}(V, W)$ and $B = \{v_i \mid i \in I\}$ a basis of $V$, $\phi: V \to W$ is **uniquely determined** by the values $\phi(v_i) \in W$. More explicitly: know the values of $\phi(v_i)$ for all $i \in I$ $\Rightarrow$ know the value of $\phi(v)$ ($\forall v \in V$).
3. E.g. for a vector space $V$ with basis $B = \{v_i \mid i \in I\}$, we define $\phi_i \in V^* = \operatorname{Hom}(V, \mathbb{F})$ by $\phi_i(v_j) = \delta_{ij}$.
   e.g. $V = \mathbb{F}^n$, $B = \{e_1,\dots,e_n\}$: $\phi_i: \mathbb{F}^n \to \mathbb{F}$ is given by $\phi_i(e_i) = 1$, $\phi_i(e_j) = 0$ $\forall i \ne j$
   $\Rightarrow \forall v = (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)^T \in \mathbb{F}^n$, $\phi_i(v) = \phi_i(\alpha_1 e_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n e_n) = \alpha_i$
   $\Rightarrow \phi_i\big((\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)^T\big) = \alpha_i$ and $B^* = \{\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n\}$.

**Prop.** $B^* = \{\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n\}$ (对于上 ③ 的 $\phi_i$) is linearly independent.

*Proof.* Take any ($\forall$) finite subset $\{\phi_{i_1},\dots,\phi_{i_k}\} \subseteq B^*$. Consider $\exists \beta_1,\dots,\beta_k$ not all zeros with $\beta_1\phi_{i_1} + \cdots + \beta_k\phi_{i_k} = \mathbf{0}_{V^*}: V \to \mathbb{F}$. Evaluate both sides at $v_{i_j}$, $\forall j \in [1,k]$ … $\Rightarrow \forall j, \beta_j = 0 \Rightarrow$ contradiction.

**Corollary.** If $\dim(V) = n < \infty$, then $B^*$ is a basis of $V^*$.

*Proof.* $\dim(V^*) = \dim(\operatorname{Hom}(V, \mathbb{F})) \overset{\text{HW4}}{=} \dim(V)\cdot\dim(\mathbb{F}) = \dim(V)\cdot 1 = n$. Since $\dim(V^*) = n$ and $B^* = \{\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n\}$ is a set of $n$ l.i. vectors in $V^*$, $B^*$ must be a basis of $V^*$.

## Dual Basis

**Def.** If $\dim(V) < \infty$, then $B^*$ is called a **dual basis** of $V^*$.

**Remark.** If $\dim(V) = \infty$, then $B^*$ is still l.i., but **not** a basis of $V^*$.

**E.g.** $V = P(\mathbb{R})$, $B = \{x^i \mid i \in \mathbb{N}\}$ $\Rightarrow B^* = \{\phi_i \mid i \in \mathbb{N}\}$ is l.i., but $\operatorname{Span}(B^*) \ne V^*$.

Consider $\psi \in V^*$ defined by $\psi(p(x)) = p(1)$. Claim: $\psi \notin \operatorname{Span}(B^*)$.
Suppose on contrary $\psi = \gamma_1\phi_{i_1} + \cdots + \gamma_k\phi_{i_k} \in \operatorname{Span}(B^*)$; W.L.O.G. let $i_1 < i_2 < \cdots < i_k$. Consider $x^{i_k + 1} \in V$ and evaluate (证 L = R):
(LHS) $\psi(x^{i_k+1}) = 1^{i_k+1} = 1$; (RHS) $\gamma_1\phi_{i_1}(x^{i_k+1}) + \cdots = 0 + \cdots + 0 = 0$ $\Rightarrow$ ≠, cannot hold.

($\Rightarrow V^*$ is "bigger" than $V$ in $\infty$-dim spaces; e.g. 见 …)

## Annihilators

**Def.** For a vector space $V$ and subset $S \subseteq V$:

$$\operatorname{Ann}(S) = \{f \in V^* \mid f(s) = 0,\ \forall s \in S\}\quad(\Rightarrow \operatorname{Ann}(S) \subseteq V^*)$$

**E.g.** $V = M_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{R})$, $B = \{E_{11}, E_{12}, E_{21}, E_{22}\} \subseteq V$, $B^* = \{\phi_{11}, \phi_{12}, \phi_{21}, \phi_{22}\} \subseteq V^*$.
Let $S = \left\{\begin{pmatrix}1 & 2\\ 0 & -1\end{pmatrix}\right\} \subseteq V$ (设 $A$ = this matrix). Then $\phi_{11}(A) = 1$, $\phi_{12}(A) = 2$, $\phi_{21}(A) = 0$, $\phi_{22}(A) = -1$.
Then $\phi_{21} \in \operatorname{Ann}(S)$, $\phi_{11} + \phi_{22} \in \operatorname{Ann}(S)$, $2\phi_{11} - \phi_{12} \in \operatorname{Ann}(S)$, …

**Prop. 5.2.**

1. The set $\operatorname{Ann}(S)$ is a vector subspace of $V^*$.
   *Proof.* Let $\phi_1, \phi_2 \in \operatorname{Ann}(S) \subseteq V^*$ (WTS: $\alpha\phi_1 + \beta\phi_2 \in \operatorname{Ann}(S)$). Take any $s \in S$: $(\alpha\phi_1 + \beta\phi_2)(s) = \alpha\cdot 0 + \beta\cdot 0 = 0$.
2. The mapping $\operatorname{Ann}(\cdot)$ is inclusion-reversing, i.e. $W_1 \subseteq W_2 \Rightarrow \operatorname{Ann}(W_2) \subseteq \operatorname{Ann}(W_1)$.
   *Proof.* Take $\phi \in \operatorname{Ann}(W_2)$ and consider $\forall w_1 \in W_1$. Then $\phi(w_1) = 0$ (∵ $w_1 \in W_1 \subseteq W_2$, $\phi \in \operatorname{Ann}(W_2)$) $\Rightarrow \phi \in \operatorname{Ann}(W_1)$.
3. $\operatorname{Ann}(S) = \operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Span}(S))$.
   *Proof.* ① By (2), $S \subseteq \operatorname{Span}(S) \Rightarrow \operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Span}(S)) \subseteq \operatorname{Ann}(S)$.
   ② (WTS: $\operatorname{Ann}(S) \subseteq \operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Span}(S))$.) Take $\forall\phi \in \operatorname{Ann}(S)$ and $\sum_{i=1}^k \beta_i s_i \in \operatorname{Span}(S)$:
   $\phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^k\beta_i s_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^k\beta_i\phi(s_i) = \sum_{i=1}^k\beta_i\cdot 0 = 0 \Rightarrow \phi \in \operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Span}(S))$.
4. $\dim(V) < \infty$, $W \le V$ $\Rightarrow \dim(W) + \dim(\operatorname{Ann}(W)) = \dim(V)$.
   *Proof.* Let $\{w_1,\dots,w_k\}$ be a basis of $W$; (Basis Extension) $\{w_1,\dots,w_k,v_{k+1},\dots,v_n\}$ a basis of $V$ $\Rightarrow B^* = \{\phi_1,\dots,\phi_k,\phi_{k+1},\dots,\phi_n\}$ the dual basis of $V^*$.
   **Claim:** $\operatorname{Ann}(W) = \operatorname{Span}\{\phi_{k+1},\dots,\phi_n\}$. If the claim holds, then $\dim(W) + \dim(\operatorname{Ann}(W)) = k + (n - k) = n = \dim(V)$.
   证 claim 成立:
   (⊇) $\forall\beta_{k+1}\phi_{k+1} + \cdots + \beta_n\phi_n \in \operatorname{Span}\{\phi_{k+1},\dots,\phi_n\}$, by dual basis, $\forall 1 \le i \le k$, $(\cdots)(w_i) = 0$.
   (⊆) To check the other inclusion, take $\forall\phi \in \operatorname{Ann}(\{w_1,\dots,w_k\})$; write $\phi = \alpha_1\phi_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n\phi_n$
   $\Rightarrow 0 = \phi(w_i) = \alpha_1\phi_1(w_i) + \cdots + \alpha_n\phi_n(w_i) = \alpha_i$ ($i \le k$)
   $\Rightarrow \phi = \alpha_{k+1}\phi_{k+1} + \cdots + \alpha_n\phi_n \in \operatorname{Span}\{\phi_{k+1},\dots,\phi_n\}$.

**E.g.** $V = M_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{R})$, $S' = \left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\right\}$, $W = \operatorname{Span}(S')$.
Then (by (3)) $\operatorname{Ann}(S') = \operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Span}(S'))$ $\Rightarrow$ the dimensions are equal (… 见 …).
