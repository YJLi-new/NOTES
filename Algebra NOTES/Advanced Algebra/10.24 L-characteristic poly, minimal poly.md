Linear operator: $T: V \to V$.

## Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors

**Def.** $T(v) = \lambda v \iff v \in \ker(T - \lambda I)$ → $v$: an eigenvector of $T$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$.

## Characteristic polynomial

**Def.** The characteristic polynomial $\chi_T(x)$ of $T: V \to V$ is $\chi_T(x) := \det((T)_{AA} - xI)$ ($A = \{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$ is a basis of $V$).

**Remark.** If we use $B = \{v_1',\dots,v_n'\} \ne A$, then $T_{AA} \ne T_{BB}$, but $\det((T)_{AA} - xI) = \det((T)_{BB} - xI)$, so $\chi_T(x)$ does not depend on the choice of basis.

证: $T_{AA} = \mathcal{C}_{AB}T_{BB}\mathcal{C}_{BA}$ (已知). Let $C = \mathcal{C}_{BA}$:

$$\det(T_{AA} - xI) = \det(C^{-1}T_{BB}C - xC^{-1}IC) = \det(C^{-1}(T_{BB} - xI)C) = \frac{1}{\det C}\det(T_{BB} - xI)\det C = \det(T_{BB} - xI)$$

**E.g.** $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $T\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\ \beta\end{pmatrix} := \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\ \beta\end{pmatrix}$, $B = \{(1,0)^T, (0,1)^T\} \Rightarrow T_{BB} = \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}$. Then

$$\chi_T(x) = \det(T_{BB} - xI) = \det\begin{pmatrix}1 - x & 2\\ 3 & 4 - x\end{pmatrix} = x^2 - 5x - 2$$

(★ here $I: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $I = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$.) (Ex.: 证 with $A = \{(1,1)^T, (1,-1)^T\}$.)

(推) 能通过解 $\chi_T(x) = 0$ 来找到所有 eigenvalues / eigenvectors of $T: V \to V$. 过程:

Suppose $\chi_T(\lambda) = 0$
$\iff \det(T_{AA} - \lambda I) = 0$
$\iff \exists w \in \mathbb{F}^n\setminus\{0\}$ s.t. $T_{AA}w = \lambda w$ (in MAT2040)
$\iff [T(v)]_A = \lambda[v]_A$ (let $v \in V$ s.t. $[v]_A = w \in \mathbb{F}^n$)
$\iff [T(v) - \lambda v]_A = \mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{F}^n}$
$\iff T(v) = \lambda v$, i.e. $v$ is an eigenvector of $T$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$.

## Linear operators induced from polynomials

**Def.** $T: V \to V$ is a linear operator, $p(x) = a_nx^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \cdots + a_1x + a_0 \in \mathbb{F}[x]$. Define

$$p(T) = a_nT^n + \cdots + a_2T^2 + a_1T + a_0I$$

where $T^n = T\circ T\circ\cdots\circ T$ ($n$ times), $T^2 = T\circ T: V \to V$, and $I: V \to V$ is the identity operator.

**E.g.** $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $T\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\ \beta\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\ \beta\end{pmatrix}$, $f(x) = x^2 + 4$:

$$f(T) = \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}^2 + 4I = \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix} + 4\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}11&10\\15&26\end{pmatrix},\qquad \chi_T(T) = \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$$

**Remark.** Compute $(f(T))_{BB}$ for $B = \{(1,0)^T, (0,1)^T\}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$: $f(T) = T^2 + 4I = T\circ T + 4I: V \to V$. Then

$$(f(T))_{BB} = (T\circ T)_{BB} + 4(I)_{BB} = (T)_{BB}(T)_{BB} + 4I = \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}^2 + 4\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}11&10\\15&26\end{pmatrix}$$

and $(\chi_T(T))_{BB} = \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix} \Rightarrow \chi_T(T): V \to V$ maps all $v \mapsto \mathbf{0}$.

## Cayley–Hamilton Theorem

$\chi_T(T) = \mathbf{0}_{V\to V}$ for all $T: V \to V$ ($\dim V < \infty$).

## Minimal polynomial

**Def.** $T: V \to V$. $m_T(x) \in \mathbb{F}[x]$ is a **minimal polynomial** $\Leftarrow$

1. $m_T(x)$ is monic,
2. $m_T(T) = \mathbf{0}_{V\to V}$,
3. $m_T(x)$ is of the smallest possible degree satisfying ① & ②.

**E.g.** $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ as above. $q(x) = x^2 - 5x - 2$ (monic) satisfies $q(T) = \mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2}$, $\deg q = 2$. (Claim: $m_T(x) = q(x)$.)
Suppose (on contrary) ∃ monic poly $s(x) = x - k$ s.t. $s(T) = \mathbf{0}$ and $\deg s < \deg q$. However: (见 … 10.24)

1. **For $T: V \to V$, $\dim(V) < \infty$, $m_T(x)$ always exists.**

   *Proof.* Consider $X := \operatorname{Hom}(V, V)$; then $\dim(X) = \dim(V)\cdot\dim(V) = n^2$. Consider $\{I, T, T^2, T^3, \dots, T^{n^2}\} \subseteq X$ ($n^2 + 1$ operators): it is l.d., i.e. $\exists\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_{n^2}$ (scalars, not all zeros) with
   $$\alpha_0I + \alpha_1T + \cdots + \alpha_{n^2}T^{n^2} = 0$$
   Suppose that the last nonzero term is $\alpha_m$. Then $\alpha_0I + \alpha_1T + \cdots + \alpha_mT^m = \mathbf{0}$
   $$\Rightarrow T^m + \frac{\alpha_{m-1}}{\alpha_m}T^{m-1} + \cdots + \frac{\alpha_1}{\alpha_m}T + \frac{\alpha_0}{\alpha_m}I = \mathbf{0}$$
   Consider (见 …): a monic polynomial annihilating $T$ exists, so one of smallest degree exists.
