**Theorem.** $T: V \to V$ is $\triangle$-able $\iff \chi_T(x) = (x - \lambda_1)\cdots(x - \lambda_n)$.

**Corollary.** $V$ is a $\mathbb{C}$ (complex) vector space $\Rightarrow \forall T: V \to V$ is $\triangle$-able.
*Proof.* By the fundamental theorem of algebra, $\forall\chi_T(x)$ can be factorized linearly.

**Theorem 1.** $T: V \to V$ is $\triangle$-able $\Rightarrow \chi_T(T) = \mathbf{0}_{V\to V}$.

*Proof.* Suppose ∃ a basis $B$ (of $V$) s.t. $T_{BB} = \begin{pmatrix}\lambda_1 & & *\\ & \ddots & \\ 0 & & \lambda_n\end{pmatrix}$.
Consider $\chi_T(x) = (x - \lambda_1)\cdots(x - \lambda_n)$ and study $\chi_T(T) = (T - \lambda_1I)\cdots(T - \lambda_nI): V \to V$ (and check $= \mathbf{0}_{V\to V}$):

$$\chi_T(T)_{BB} = (T_{BB} - \lambda_1I_{BB})\cdots(T_{BB} - \lambda_nI_{BB}) \in M_{n\times n}\quad(\text{check } = 0_{n\times n})$$

$$= \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}0 & & *\\ & \lambda_2 - \lambda_1 & \\ 0 & & \lambda_n - \lambda_1\end{pmatrix}}_{A_1}\cdots\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_1 - \lambda_n & & *\\ & \lambda_{n-1} - \lambda_n & \\ 0 & & 0\end{pmatrix}}_{A_n}$$

**Claim.** $\forall (x_1,\dots,x_n)^T \in \mathbb{F}^n$, $(A_1\cdots A_n)(x_1,\dots,x_n)^T = \mathbf{0}$ (so $A_1\cdots A_n = 0_{n\times n}$).

*Proof of claim.* $A_n$ has zero last row, so $A_n(x_1,\dots,x_n)^T = (x_1',\dots,x_{n-1}',0)^T$; then $A_{n-1}$ kills the $(n-1)$-th coordinate: $A_1\cdots A_{n-1}(x_1',\dots,x_{n-1}',0)^T = A_1\cdots A_{n-2}(x_1'',\dots,x_{n-2}'',0,0)^T = \cdots = \mathbf{0}$.

**Corollary 2 (C.-H. for $\mathbb{C}$-vector spaces).** $\forall T: V \to V$, where $V$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space $\Rightarrow \chi_T(T) = \mathbf{0}_{V\to V} \Rightarrow m_T(x) \mid \chi_T(x)$.

**Corollary (C.-H. for $\mathbb{R}$-vector spaces).** Let $T: V \to V$ be any linear operator, where $V$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-vector space. Then $\chi_T(T) = \mathbf{0}_{V\to V} \Rightarrow m_T(x) \mid \chi_T(x)$.
(Note: not all $T: V \to V$ ($V$ an $\mathbb{R}$-space) are $\triangle$-able, so Thm 1 doesn't apply directly.)

*Proof.* Let $B$ be any (real) basis of $V$. Consider $M := T_{BB} \in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R})$. Then $\chi_T(x) = \chi_M(x)$ (defined in MAT2040).
Consider $M: \mathbb{C}^n \to \mathbb{C}^n$, $x \mapsto Mx$ (2040). ($\mathbb{C}^n$ uses the usual basis $\mathcal{E} = \{e_1,\dots,e_n\}$, $M_{\mathcal{EE}} = M$.)
$\Rightarrow \chi_M(x)$ (3040) $= \chi_M(x)$ (2040).
Apply Corollary 2 (C.-H. for $\mathbb{C}$-vector spaces) on $M$: $\chi_M(M) = \mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{C}^n\to\mathbb{C}^n} \Rightarrow \chi_M(M) = 0$ (2040) $\Rightarrow$ … (见 …)

**Theorem 3.** Let $T: V \to V$, $V$ a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space, with $\chi_T(x) = (x - \lambda_1)^{e_1}\cdots(x - \lambda_k)^{e_k}$ (recall: the irreducible $p_i(x) \in \mathbb{C}[x]$ are linear, $p_i(x) = x - \lambda_i$). Then

$$m_T(x) = (x - \lambda_1)^{f_1}\cdots(x - \lambda_k)^{f_k}\quad\text{with } 1 \le f_i \le e_i,\ \forall i$$

*Proof.* Consider $\chi_T(x) = (x - \alpha)^{e_1}(x - \beta)^{e_2}$. Suppose on contrary $m_T(x) = 1\cdot(x - \beta)^{f_2}$ (i.e. the factor $(x - \alpha)$ is missing).
Then (by $\chi_T(x)$) ∃ eigenvector $v \ne \mathbf{0}$ with (1) $T(v) = \alpha v$, and (by def. of $m_T(x)$) (2) $(T - \beta I)^{f_2} = \mathbf{0}_{V\to V}$. So

$$(2) \Rightarrow \mathbf{0}_{V\to V}(v) = (T - \beta I)^{f_2}(v) \Rightarrow \mathbf{0}_V = \underbrace{(T - \beta I)\cdots(T - \beta I)}_{f_2}(v) = (T - \beta I)\cdots(T - \beta I)(Tv - \beta v)$$

$\Rightarrow$ (using (1)) $= (\alpha - \beta)^{f_2}v \ne \mathbf{0}$ — contradiction.
