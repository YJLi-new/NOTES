## JNF (cont.)

**Case 1.** $\chi_T(x) = x^n$, $m_T(x) = x^m$ $\Rightarrow$ ∃ basis $B$ of $V$ s.t. $(T)_{B,B}$ is block diagonal ($n\times n$) with nilpotent Jordan blocks (0 on the diagonal, 1 on the superdiagonal).

- There is always (∃) one $m\times m$ Jordan block.
- The sizes of the other blocks: found by $\dim(\ker(T^i))$, $1 \le i \le m$ (见本周 Tut).
- The number of (all) blocks $= \dim(\ker(T))$.

**Case 2.** $m_T(x) = (x - \mu)^m$. Consider $S: V \to V$, $S := T - \mu\,\mathrm{Id}$. Then $m_S(x) = x^m$ ⇒ Case 1 gives a basis with $(S)_{B,B}$ in nilpotent Jordan form, and $(S)_{B,B} = (T)_{B,B} - \mu(I)_{B,B}$
⇒ $(T)_{B,B}$ = the same block structure with $\mu$ on the diagonal (blocks $\begin{pmatrix}\mu & 1 & \\ & \ddots & 1\\ & & \mu\end{pmatrix}$, the largest of size $m$).

**Case 3.** $m_T(x) = (x - \mu_1)^{e_1}\cdots(x - \mu_k)^{e_k}$. Apply PDT, where

- $V_i = \ker(T - \mu_i\,\mathrm{Id})^{e_i}$,
- $V_i$ are $T$-invariant,
- for $T|_{V_i}: V_i \to V_i$, $m_{T|_{V_i}}(x) = (x - \mu_i)^{e_i}$.

$V = V_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_i \oplus \cdots \oplus V_k$ with basis $B := B_1 \cup \cdots \cup B_i \cup \cdots \cup B_k$. Then

$$(T)_{B,B} = \begin{pmatrix}M_1 & & \\ & M_2 & \\ & & \ddots & \\ & & & M_k\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_i = (T|_{V_i})_{B_i,B_i}\text{ with } m_{T|_{V_i}}(x) = (x - \mu_i)^{e_i}$$

By Case 2, choose $B_i$ s.t. $M_i$ is in Jordan form with diagonal $\mu_i$ ⇒ $(T)_{B,B}$ is in JNF.

## Inner Product Space

(Goal: generalize the dot product in $\mathbb{R}^n$.)

### Bilinear form for an $\mathbb{R}$-vector space $V$

**Def.** $V$: a vector space over $\mathbb{R}$. A bilinear form on $V$ is a mapping $F: V \times V \to \mathbb{R}$ with

1. $F(u + v, w) = F(u, w) + F(v, w)$
2. $F(u, v + w) = F(u, v) + F(u, w)$
3. $F(\lambda u, v) = F(u, \lambda v) = \lambda F(u, v)$

E.g. write 3. as $\langle\lambda u, v\rangle = \langle u, \lambda v\rangle = \lambda\langle u, v\rangle$ ($\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$).

**E.g.**

- (a) $V = \mathbb{R}^n$, $\langle u, v\rangle := u^tv = (u_1 \cdots u_n)(v_1,\dots,v_n)^T = u_1v_1 + \cdots + u_nv_n$ (usual dot product; $p = n$, $q = 0$ below).
- (b) $V = \mathbb{R}^n$, $n = p + q$: $\langle u, v\rangle_{p,q} := (u_1v_1 + \cdots + u_pv_p) - (u_{p+1}v_{p+1} + \cdots + u_nv_n)$. (Ex.: $\langle\,,\rangle_{p,q}$ satisfies 1–3.)
- (c) $V = C^\infty([0,1])$: for $f, g$, $\langle f, g\rangle := \int_0^1 f(x)g(x)\,dx$ ⇒ $\langle f, f\rangle = \int_0^1 f^2(x)\,dx \ge 0$ ⇒ positive definite.

**Def.** $\langle\,,\rangle$

- is **symmetric** $\iff \langle u, v\rangle = \langle v, u\rangle$;
- is **positive definite** $\iff \forall v$, $\langle v, v\rangle \ge 0$, and $\langle v, v\rangle = 0 \iff v = \mathbf{0}$;
- is **nondegenerate** $\iff (\langle u, v\rangle = 0,\ \forall v \Rightarrow u = \mathbf{0})$.

**Remark.** Positive definite ⇒ nondegenerate.
*Proof.* Suppose $u$ satisfies $\forall v \in V$, $\langle u, v\rangle = 0$. Then take $v = u$ ⇒ $\langle u, u\rangle = 0 \iff u = \mathbf{0}$.

### Bilinear form for a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space

We can define a bilinear $\langle\,,\rangle$ on a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space $V$, but such a form is **never** positive definite.
证: Suppose $\langle v, v\rangle > 0$. Then $\langle iv, iv\rangle = (-1)\langle v, v\rangle < 0$.

### Sesquilinear form

$V$: a vector space over $\mathbb{C}$. A sesquilinear form on $V$: a mapping $F: V \times V \to \mathbb{C}$ with

1. $F(u + v, w) = F(u, w) + F(v, w)$
2. $F(u, v + w) = F(u, v) + F(u, w)$
3. $F(\lambda u, v) = \bar\lambda F(u, v)$, $F(u, \lambda v) = \lambda F(u, v)$, i.e. $\langle\lambda u, v\rangle = \bar\lambda\langle u, v\rangle$ and $\langle u, \lambda v\rangle = \lambda\langle u, v\rangle$.

Suppose $\langle v, v\rangle > 0$. Then $\langle iv, iv\rangle = (-i\cdot i)\langle v, v\rangle = 1\cdot\langle v, v\rangle > 0$.

It is **conjugate-symmetric** $\iff \langle u, v\rangle = \overline{\langle v, u\rangle}$.
E.g. $V = \mathbb{C}^n$, $\langle u, v\rangle := \bar u_1v_1 + \cdots + \bar u_nv_n$.

### Inner product space

**Def.**

- For an $\mathbb{R}$-vector space: bilinear, symmetric, positive definite.
- For a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space: sesquilinear, conjugate symmetric, positive definite.

Let $(V, \langle\,,\rangle)$ be an inner product space.

1. $\|v\| := \sqrt{\langle v, v\rangle}$ is the **norm** of $v$.
2. $\|v\| = 1 \iff v$ is a **unit vector**.
3. $u, v$ are **orthogonal** ($u \perp v$) $\iff \langle u, v\rangle = 0$.

**Prop. (Gram–Schmidt process).** $\{v_1,\dots,v_k\}$ is linearly independent ⇒ ∃ $\{w_1,\dots,w_k\}$ s.t. $\forall i$, $\|w_i\| = 1$; $\forall i \ne j$, $\langle w_i, w_j\rangle = 0$; $\operatorname{Span}\{v_1,\dots,v_k\} = \operatorname{Span}\{w_1,\dots,w_k\}$; given by

$$w_1 = \frac{v_1}{\|v_1\|},\qquad w_{i+1} = \frac{w_{i+1}'}{\|w_{i+1}'\|},\quad w_{i+1}' := v_{i+1} - \big(\langle w_1, v_{i+1}\rangle w_1 + \cdots + \langle w_i, v_{i+1}\rangle w_i\big)$$

## Orthogonal Complement

**Def.** Let $U \subseteq V$ be a subset. Then the orthogonal complement of $U$ is $U^\perp = \{v \in V \mid \langle v, u\rangle = 0,\ \forall u \in U\}$.

**Prop.**

- (a) $U^\perp \le V$ is a vector subspace.
- (b) $U \subseteq (U^\perp)^\perp$.
- (c) $\dim(V) < \infty$, $W \le V$ ⇒ $\dim(W) + \dim(W^\perp) = \dim(V)$ and $W \oplus W^\perp = V$.

*Proof.*

- (a) Let $w_1, w_2 \in U^\perp$ ⇒ $\forall u \in U$, $\langle aw_1 + bw_2, u\rangle = \langle aw_1, u\rangle + \langle bw_2, u\rangle = \bar a\langle w_1, u\rangle + \bar b\langle w_2, u\rangle = 0 + 0 = 0$.
- (b) Let $w \in U$. $\forall x \in U^\perp$, $\langle w, x\rangle = 0$ ⇒ $w \in (U^\perp)^\perp$.
- (c) Let $\{w_1,\dots,w_k\}$ be a basis of $W$; basis extension → $\{w_1,\dots,w_k,v_{k+1},\dots,v_n\}$ a basis of $V$; Gram–Schmidt → $\{u_1,\dots,u_k,u_{k+1},\dots,u_n\}$ an orthogonal basis of $V$, where $u_1,\dots,u_k$ is an orthogonal basis of $W$. (HW: $W^\perp = \operatorname{Span}\{u_{k+1},\dots,u_n\}$.)
