## Change of basis matrix

**Prop.** $A = \{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$, $A' = \{w_1,\dots,w_n\}$, $v_j = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{ij} w_i$. Then

$$(*)\qquad \mathcal{C}_{A'A}[v]_A = [v]_{A'} \in \mathbb{F}^n,\quad (v \in V)$$

*Proof.* (★ ① 指定任意特值, ② generalize ①)

① Check $(*)$ holds for $v = v_j$:
LHS $= \mathcal{C}_{A'A}[v_j]_A = \mathcal{C}_{A'A}[0v_1 + \cdots + 1v_j + \cdots + 0v_n]_A = \mathcal{C}_{A'A}\, e_j = j$-th col. of $\mathcal{C}_{A'A} = (\alpha_{1j},\dots,\alpha_{nj})^T$.
RHS $= [v_j]_{A'} \overset{(**)}{=} \left[\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{ij} w_i\right]_{A'} = (\alpha_{1j},\dots,\alpha_{nj})^T$ $(=$ LHS$)$.

② $\forall v = k_1 v_1 + \cdots + k_n v_n$:

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{C}_{A'A}[v]_A &= \mathcal{C}_{A'A}(k_1[v_1]_A + \cdots + k_n[v_n]_A) && ([\ ]_A: V \to \mathbb{F}^n \text{ is an isom.})\\
&= k_1\mathcal{C}_{A'A}[v_1]_A + \cdots + k_n\mathcal{C}_{A'A}[v_n]_A && (\text{shown in MAT2040})\\
&= k_1[v_1]_{A'} + \cdots + k_n[v_n]_{A'} && (\text{Step ①})\\
&= [k_1 v_1 + \cdots + k_n v_n]_{A'} = [v]_{A'}
\end{aligned}$$

## Matrix Representation of a Linear Transformation

**Def.** Have known: $v \in V$, $[v]_A \in \mathbb{F}^n$. WTS: $T: V \to W$, $T_{BA} \in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{F})$.

| | $V$ | $\xrightarrow{T}$ | $W$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| dim | $n$ | | $m$ |
| basis | $A = \{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$ | | $B = \{w_1,\dots,w_m\}$ |

$$(T)_{BA} := (\alpha_{ij}) \in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{F}),\qquad \text{where } T(v_j) = \sum_{i=1}^m \alpha_{ij}w_i$$

**E.g.** $T: P_3(\mathbb{R}) \to P_1(\mathbb{R})$, $T(p) = p''$; dim $4 \to 2$; bases $A = \{x^2, 1, x^3, x\}$, $B = \{x, 1\}$.

- $T(x^2) = (x^2)'' = (2x)' = 2 = 0\cdot x + 2\cdot 1$
- $T(1) = 0x + 0\cdot 1$
- $T(x^3) = 6x + 0\cdot 1$
- $T(x) = 0x + 0\cdot 1$

$$\Rightarrow T_{BA} = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & 6 & 0\\ 2 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{pmatrix} \in M_{2\times 4}(\mathbb{R})\quad(\text{注意: 方向、顺序})$$

**Theorem 1.** If $T: V_A \to W_B$, then $T_{BA}[v]_A = [T(v)]_B$ (可视为 matrix–vector multiplication).

*Proof.* Let $A = \{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$ be a basis of $V$, $B = \{w_1,\dots,w_m\}$ a basis of $W$.
① Check Thm 1 holds for $v = v_j$: LHS $= T_{BA}[v_j]_A = T_{BA}e_j = j$-th col. of $T_{BA} = (\alpha_{1j},\dots,\alpha_{mj})^T$; RHS $= [T(v_j)]_B \overset{(***)}{=} \left[\sum_{i=1}^m \alpha_{ij}w_i\right]_B =$ LHS.
② For general $v = k_1 v_1 + \cdots + k_n v_n \in V$: … Ex.: same proof as in the change-of-basis-matrix prop.

**Theorem 2.** $V \xrightarrow{T} W \xrightarrow{S} U$ with $\dim = n, m, p$ and bases $A = \{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$, $B = \{w_1,\dots,w_m\}$, $\mathcal{C} = \{u_1,\dots,u_p\}$; $T_{BA}$: $m\times n$, $S_{\mathcal{C}B}$: $p\times m$, $(S\circ T)_{\mathcal{C}A}$: $p\times n$. Then

$$\underbrace{(S\circ T)_{\mathcal{C}A}}_{\text{composition of l. trans.}} = \underbrace{S_{\mathcal{C}B}\cdot T_{BA}}_{\text{matrix mult.}}\quad(\text{relationship} ☆)$$

*Proof.* ① Compare the $j$-th col. of $(S\circ T)_{\mathcal{C}A}$ and $S_{\mathcal{C}B}T_{BA}$. With $(****)$ $S(w_i) = \sum_{k=1}^p \beta_{ki}u_k$, i.e. $S_{\mathcal{C}B} = (\beta_{ki})$:

$$\begin{aligned}
(S\circ T)_{\mathcal{C}A}e_j &= (S\circ T)_{\mathcal{C}A}[v_j]_A \overset{\text{Thm 1}}{=} [(S\circ T)(v_j)]_{\mathcal{C}} = [S(T(v_j))]_{\mathcal{C}}\\
&\overset{(***)}{=} \Big[S\Big(\sum_{i=1}^m \alpha_{ij}w_i\Big)\Big]_{\mathcal{C}} = \Big[\sum_{i=1}^m \alpha_{ij}S(w_i)\Big]_{\mathcal{C}} \overset{(****)}{=} \Big[\sum_{k=1}^p\sum_{i=1}^m \alpha_{ij}\beta_{ki}u_k\Big]_{\mathcal{C}}\\
&= \begin{pmatrix}\sum_i \alpha_{ij}\beta_{1i}\\ \vdots\\ \sum_i \alpha_{ij}\beta_{pi}\end{pmatrix} = (S_{\mathcal{C}B}T_{BA})_{\cdot j} = j\text{-th col. of } S_{\mathcal{C}B}T_{BA}
\end{aligned}$$

**Remark.** $[v]_{A'}$ and $[v]_A$ are related by the change of basis matrices $\mathcal{C}_{A'A}$ & $\mathcal{C}_{AA'}$. For $T: V \to W$, how are $[T]_{BA}$ & $[T]_{B'A'}$ related?

**Corollary.** $T_{B'A'} = \mathcal{C}_{B'B}\, T_{BA}\, \mathcal{C}_{AA'}$.

*Proof 1.* Let $A = \{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$, $A' = \{v_1',\dots\}$ be bases of $V$; $B = \{w_1,\dots,w_m\}$, $B' = \{w_1',\dots\}$ bases of $W$.
① Check 两边相等 by applying to $e_j = [v_j']_{A'}$ (指定任意特值; 先化 $e_j$):
RHS: $\mathcal{C}_{B'B}T_{BA}\mathcal{C}_{AA'}e_j = \mathcal{C}_{B'B}T_{BA}\mathcal{C}_{AA'}[v_j']_{A'} = \mathcal{C}_{B'B}T_{BA}[v_j']_A \overset{\text{Thm 1}}{=} \mathcal{C}_{B'B}[T(v_j')]_B = [T(v_j')]_{B'}$.
LHS: $T_{B'A'}e_j = T_{B'A'}[v_j']_{A'} = [T(v_j')]_{B'} =$ RHS.

*Proof 2.*

$$V_{A'} \xrightarrow{\operatorname{Id}_V} V_A \xrightarrow{T} W_B \xrightarrow{\operatorname{Id}_W} W_{B'}\ \Rightarrow\ T = \operatorname{Id}_W \circ T \circ \operatorname{Id}_V$$

By Theorem 2, $T_{B'A'} = (\operatorname{Id}_W)_{B'B}\cdot T_{BA}\cdot(\operatorname{Id}_V)_{AA'} = \mathcal{C}_{B'B}\cdot T_{BA}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{AA'}$ (in hw).
